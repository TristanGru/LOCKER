## Q1. MILSTRIP document number, Julian date, serial, and the afloat DoDAAC convention

### Takeaway
The 14-character layout (rp 30-35 DoDAAC + rp 36-39 ordinal date YDDD + rp 40-43 serial) is **CONFIRMED**, and `6308` = 4 Nov 2026 is correct. The convention "afloat DoDAAC = R + UIC" is **PARTLY WRONG**. Navy sources give **R = Pacific Fleet operating units, V = Atlantic Fleet operating units, N = shore and non-fleet activities (including precommissioning ships)**. The squadron is Norfolk-based under CNSL (Atlantic), so by that convention its DoDAACs and document numbers should start with **V**, not R. The serial `A001` (alpha first character) has no source support for afloat surface-ship use.

### Cited Findings
- **Spec claim (00 §4, 04 §4.1): "MILSTRIP document number = DoDAAC(6) + Julian date YDDD(4) + serial(4) = 14", tagged [UNV]. CONFIRMED.**
  - DLA DLMS MILSTRIP material puts the DoDAAC (or FEDSTRIP activity address code) in rp 30-35, a four-position ordinal date in rp 36-39 and the serial number in rp 40-43. The whole field is a nonduplicative 14-position document number. [snippet] — [DLA AVN MAP milstrip](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/Aviation/MAPPING/AVN_MAP_milstrip.pdf); [DLM 4000.25-1 MILSTRIP (DLA)](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/Manuals/DLM/MILSTRIP/MILSTRIP.pdf)
  - A Navy training manual describes the document number (card columns 30-43) as "a 14-digit nonduplicative number": col 30 service code, cols 31-35 the five-character UIC, cols 36-39 Julian date, cols 40-43 serial. [dated, ~1988] — [tpub, Aviation Ordnanceman 2&3, "Requisition Document Preparation"](https://www.tpub.com/aviord321/171.htm)
  - **Recommendation:** change the tag from [UNV] to [S-SNIP]/[DATED].
- **Spec claim (00 §4): "4 Nov 2026 = day 308, so YDDD = 6308". CONFIRMED.**
  - The ordinal date is "the last digit of the calendar year and the numeric consecutive day of the calendar year" (example: 8181 = 30 June 1988) — [tpub AO 2&3](https://www.tpub.com/aviord321/171.htm). DLA's mapping guide gives the same form (6001 = 1 Jan 2006) [snippet] — [DLA AVN MAP milstrip](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/Aviation/MAPPING/AVN_MAP_milstrip.pdf).
  - Arithmetic check: 2026 is not a leap year. 31 Oct is day 304, so 4 Nov is day 308. (Computed; no source needed.)
- **Spec claim (00 §3, 04 §3): "DoDAAC form 'R + UIC' for afloat units", tagged [UNV]. PARTLY WRONG.**
  - A Navy DD 1348 manual puts the service designator in rp 30: **R for Pacific Fleet operating units, V for Atlantic Fleet operating units, N for shore activities**. Fleet units may use N only when specifically authorized. [snippet, dated] — [tpub Navy DD 1348 manual](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14017/css/Dd-Form-1348-Cont-65.htm); [tpub Requisition System Document Manual DD 1348](https://medical.tpub.com/10670-c/css/Requisition-System-Document-Manual-Dd-1348-226.htm)
  - DLA's DoDAAC Assignment Logic document describes R as a mobility unit belonging to the Pacific Fleet and V as one belonging to the Atlantic Fleet. It also notes that UICs in the "20,000 series are normally ships" and the 80,000 series are normally reserves. [snippet; PDF returned 403] — [DLA DoDAAC Assignment Logic](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/Committees/DoDAAD/DoDAAC_Assignment_Logic.pdf)
  - The AO 2&3 manual lists V (Atlantic Fleet operating units), N (non-fleet Navy activities, including precommissioned ships), M (Marine Corps) and Z (Coast Guard) for column 30. It does not list R in that excerpt. [dated] — [tpub AO 2&3](https://www.tpub.com/aviord321/171.htm)
  - **Recommendation:** Kessler, Varnell and Ashby are in Norfolk under TYCOM `CNSL` (Atlantic), so use **`V99101` / `V99102` / `V99103`** and document numbers like **`V991016308xxxx`**. If you want to keep R, rehome the squadron to a Pacific port. Re-tag the convention as [S-SNIP]/[DATED] with the R/V/N meanings spelled out.
- **Spec claim (00 §4, 04 §6): example serial `A001` in `R991016308A001`. UNVERIFIABLE; likely unrealistic.**
  - Older Navy guidance calls rp 40-43 a four-position serial. Automated ships use the 5000 series and nonautomated ships the 8000 series, and the same serial must never repeat on the same Julian date. [dated] — [tpub AO 2&3](https://www.tpub.com/aviord321/171.htm)
  - In Navy aviation practice the serial starts with **W for NORS** and **G for NMCS** requisitions. So alpha first characters do exist, but with assigned meanings. [snippet, dated] — [tpub, Preparation of NORS/NMCS Requisitions](https://navyadministration.tpub.com/14241/css/Preparation-Of-Nors-Nmcs-Requisitions-113.htm)
  - I found no source for an "A" first-position serial on surface-ship requisitions.
  - **Recommendation:** use an all-numeric serial (for example `V9910163080042`) or document `A` as an invented [PH] marker. As it stands, it implies a real convention that may not exist.
- **Spec claim (00 §4): "UIC 5 chars [S]". CONFIRMED.** The UIC is the five-character requisitioner field (cols 31-35) — [tpub AO 2&3](https://www.tpub.com/aviord321/171.htm). The placeholder `991xx` sits outside the "20,000 series normally ships" range [snippet] — [DLA DoDAAC Assignment Logic](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/Committees/DoDAAD/DoDAAC_Assignment_Logic.pdf). That is acceptable for a [PH] value, but a reviewer may notice it.
- **Manual status relevant to all MILSTRIP citations: DLM 4000.25-1 (MILSTRIP) is being sunset.** ADC 1322A documents removal of MILSTRIP chapter content to support the sunset of DLM 4000.25-1. DLM 4000.25 (mainly Volume 2) is now the primary source for military requisitioning and issue procedures. [snippet] — [ADC 1322A](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC_1322A_MILSTRIP_Chapter_Removal.pdf); [ADC 1322](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC_1322_MILSTRIP_DLMS_Alignment.pdf). The MILSTRIP PDF says that for updates after 2 April 2019 the reader should see DLM 4000.25 Volume 2 [snippet] — [MILSTRIP.pdf](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/Manuals/DLM/MILSTRIP/MILSTRIP.pdf).

### Inferences
- With R→V and a numeric serial, the corrected scenario document number would be `V9910163080001` (or any 4-digit serial), with `dodaac` `V99101`. Every join key that embeds the document number (CSMP `req_doc_numbers`, scenario feed) must change in step.
- The R/V split reflects the fleet assignment when the DoDAAC was issued. Whether a ship's DoDAAC changes when it shifts fleets was not found, so a mixed squadron is defensible if documented.

### Gaps
- No current (post-2019) primary text on Navy afloat DoDAAC prefixes was retrieved. The DoDAAD service-code table (DLM 4000.25 Vol 6) and DLA's DoDAAC logic PDF were blocked (403).
- Whether R-Supply assigns serials in a fixed series today (5000/8000 is 1980s guidance) is unverified.

## Q2. Supply status codes (DLM 4000.25 Vol 2 AP7.16): meanings, mapping table, current change number

BA, BB, BC, BV, BZ, B5, CB, CG, CJ and CU are **CONFIRMED** in substance. **B3 is slightly WRONG**: the mirror defines it in terms of the RAD (required availability date) and supplies the availability date, which is not generic "RDD unrealistic". BD is missing from the spec and is worth adding. AP7.16 **Change 18 (15 Sep 2024)** is real, but it is probably **OUTDATED** as the latest: a mirror cites an AP7.16 revision of 17 Oct 2025, and DLA lists Vol 2 chapter changes dated 31 Oct 2025. The current change number could not be confirmed.

- **Spec claim (04 §2): "C-series = rejected; must be re-requisitioned". - **Related transaction:** the DLMS supply-status transaction is the **870S** (ADC 1451 references "DLMS 870S supply status X12 position information") [snippet] — [ADC 1451](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC_1451_AdminUpdate-to-AP716_MFR.pdf). The spec's "DIC AE_/AS_" mention stays **UNVERIFIABLE** (not checked this pass).

That is the legacy location before MILSTRIP content moved into Vol 2 — [ADC 1322A](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC_1322A_MILSTRIP_Chapter_Removal.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Scenario realism (04 §6, README §7/§10):** Kessler goes `BB` → `BC` with an ESD only about 10 days out (15 Nov). BC means "long delay anticipated", which in practice signals a procurement-length tail, so a 10-day ESD on a BC is internally odd. Two options: (a) keep `BB` and slip the ESD, which matches the "ESD slips to day 11" story better; or (b) keep `BC` and push the ESD weeks out, making the program-office or cross-deck fix decisive. Either is a [PROP] choice, but the BC meaning should match the ESD.
- The spec's regime mapping (`BA`=NEAR, `BB`=DUE_IN, `BC`/`BV`/`BZ` long or procurement) is consistent with the code meanings.
- Ashby X3 (a `BA` whose ESD has passed) is a plausible staleness pattern, and B5 is the code a follow-up would produce.

### Gaps
- The full AP7.16 primary text and the current Vol 2 formal change number (after Change 18) could not be retrieved; DLA returned 403.
- The DLMS ESD date format (CCYYMMDD) was not verified.

## Q3. Priority designators, Issue Priority Groups, F/AD × UND matrix, RDD 999, NORS, Time Definite Delivery

### Takeaway
PD 01-15 and IPG I/II/III = 01-03/04-08/09-15 are **CONFIRMED**, and the F/AD × UND matrix is consistent with them. "RDD 999 = C2/C3/C4 CASREP" is **CONFIRMED only as dated Navy guidance and is incomplete**. Under DoD rules, 999 is an expedited-handling signal limited to **PD 01-03 NMCS requisitions for forces overseas or units deploying within 30 days**; CONUS NMCS uses **"N" in rp 62** and anticipated NMCS uses **"E"**. "NORS" is a **dated** Navy term for CASREP requisitions; current vocabulary is NMCS/ANMCS. The TDD description is **PARTLY WRONG**: the categories are Transportation Priorities TP1/TP2/TP3, and TP2 applies only to PD 04-15.

### Cited Findings
- **Spec claim (00 §4, 04 §3): "Priority designator 01 (highest) to 15". CONFIRMED.** The PD is a two-digit code from 01 (highest) to 15 (lowest), formed from the F/AD (I-V) and the UND (A, B, C). UND A = unable to perform mission, B = impaired operational capability, C = routine. [snippet, dated] — [tpub Urgency of Need Designator](https://navyadvancement.tpub.com/12024/css/Urgency-Of-Need-Designator-133.htm)
- **F/AD × UND matrix (spec lists it as [UNV]). CONFIRMED** from a 1998 DLA table, also reproduced on tpub [snippet]:

| F/AD | UND A | UND B | UND C |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 01 | 04 | 11 |
| II | 02 | 05 | 12 |
| III | 03 | 06 | 13 |
| IV | 07 | 09 | 14 |
| V | 08 | 10 | 15 |

  — [DLA ADC 009a](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC009a.pdf). The Navy implementing instruction is OPNAVINST 4614.1 (series). A 2023 NETC instruction cites **OPNAVINST 4614.1H** [snippet] — [NETCINST 4614.1D](https://www.netc.navy.mil/Portals/46/NETC/inst/46141D.pdf).
- **Spec claim (04 §4.1): `issue_priority_group` "1 if PD 01-03, 2 if 04-08, 3 if 09-15", tagged "DATED (IPG I) / UNV (II, III splits)". CONFIRMED.** A Navy source groups PD 01-03 as Issue Group I, 04-08 as II and 09-15 as III [snippet, dated] — [tpub Requisition Handling Precedence](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/12654/css/Requisition-Handling-Precedence-131.htm). This matches the matrix: IPG II = F/AD I-III with UND B, plus F/AD IV-V with UND A. **Recommendation:** re-tag II/III from UNV to [S-SNIP]/[DATED].
- **Scenario PD `02`:** PD 02 = F/AD II + UND A per the matrix above — [DLA ADC 009a](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC009a.pdf). This is consistent with a CASREP (UND A) from a unit holding F/AD II. Whether a DDG in Norfolk in pre-deployment workups would hold F/AD II is not verified.
- **Spec claim (04 §3): "RDD 999 marks C2/C3/C4 CASREP requisitions [DATED]". CONFIRMED as dated Navy practice; INCOMPLETE/OUTDATED against DoD rules.**
  - A Navy aviation manual says 999 may be used when the requisition is a C2, C3 or C4 CASREP [snippet, dated] — [tpub MILSTRIP Message Requisition](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/12654/css/Milstrip-Message-Requisition-63.htm)
  - The same Navy text says 999 is for NMCS/PMCS/Broad Arrow PD 01-04 requisitions when the material applies to **overseas forces**, also covers activities **alerted for deployment**, and uses **"E" in rp 62** for anticipated NMCS that cannot meet the 999 criteria. **"N" in rp 62** marks NMCS for PD 01-08 [snippet, dated] — [tpub MILSTRIP Message Requisition](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/12654/css/Milstrip-Message-Requisition-63.htm)
  - A USMC UMMIPS message restricts the 999 RDD to "units operating overseas or preparing to deploy within 30 days of the requisition date" — [Marines.mil UMMIPS message](https://www.marines.mil/News/Messages/Messages-Display/Article/894054/adherence-to-the-uniform-material-movement-and-issue-prioritysystem-ummips/)
  - DLA edit rules: if 999 is present and the PD is outside 01-03, the requisition is assigned PD 03 (U.S. forces). A 999 on a requisition not intended for U.S. forces is blanked, BK status is furnished, and the requisition is processed normally. [snippet] — [ADC 057](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC057_RDDEditFinal.pdf); [ADC 057B](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC057B_RevtoRDDEdit.pdf)
  - **Recommendation:** model `rdd` as one of `999` (PD 01-03, overseas or deploying ≤30 days), `N` + days (CONUS NMCS), `E` + days (ANMCS), or a date. For Kessler (in Norfolk, COMPTUEX on day 21, deployment after that) either state that she is "alerted for deployment" to justify 999, or use `N`. Note: "N"/"E" codes are written rp 62 + two-digit days in legacy MILS; this detail is not verified.
- **Spec claim (04 §3): "CASREP orders are NORS requisitions" and the `nors` boolean, tagged [DATED]. CONFIRMED but DATED.**
  - A Navy administration manual says NORS requisitions are those submitted for a CASREP requirement, while NMCS requisitions are for aeronautical material needed to correct an aircraft NMCS condition; serials start with W (NORS) and G (NMCS) [snippet, dated] — [tpub Preparation of NORS/NMCS Requisitions](https://navyadministration.tpub.com/14241/css/Preparation-Of-Nors-Nmcs-Requisitions-113.htm)
  - Current NAVSUP material uses the NMCS term (a 2023 NAVSUP card lists an NMCS goal) [snippet] — [NAVSUP 2023 NSS Supply Elevator Card](https://www.navsup.navy.mil/Portals/65/2023%20NSS-Supply%20Elevator%20Card-DIGITAL.pdf). DoD MILSTRIP uses NMCS/ANMCS indicators (above).
  - **Recommendation:** rename `nors` to `casrep_requisition` (bool), add an `nmcs_indicator` (`N`/`E`/blank), and note that "NORS" is legacy Navy usage.
- **Spec claim (04 §3): "Time Definite Delivery: Cat 1 = PD 01-03; Cat 2 if RDD is under 8 days out (CONUS) or under 21 days (OCONUS); default CONUS in-transit 5 days [S-SNIP] (ADC 1114)". PARTLY WRONG / imprecise.**
  - DLA material (2007) defines Transportation Priority categories:
    - **TP1** = PD 01-03 with any RDD except those beginning with X or S.
    - **TP2** = **PD 04-15** with special RDD codes (444, 555, 777, N or E prefixes), or an RDD less than 8 days out (CONUS) or less than 21 days out (OCONUS).
    - **TP3** = PD 04-15 with no RDD or one beyond those thresholds.
    [snippet] — [DLA document (archive.org mirror)](https://archive.org/download/mipc-dla_mil/dla.mil_QEBANV7IVB2MNNMAJXZ62TKSBKBKYL5D.pdf)
  - ADC 1114 references the DoDM 4140.01 Vol 8 TDD standards, gives the TP2/TP3 RDD thresholds as 8 days (CONUS) and 21 days (OCONUS), and mentions a **DoDM 4140.01 default of 5 days for the CONUS in-transit segment**. Its subject is materiel receipt acknowledgment (MRA) time standards. [snippet; PDF 403] — [ADC 1114](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC_1114_Change_MRA_Times_based_on_TDD.pdf)
  - ADC 1408 also changed rules for PD 09-15 with code 777 (not read) — [ADC 1408](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC_1408_Designator_09-15_Code_777.pdf)
  - **Recommendation:** rename "Cat" to "TP1/TP2/TP3", add the PD 04-15 condition and the special-RDD codes to TP2, and describe the 5 days as "DoDM 4140.01 default CONUS in-transit segment used in MRA timeframes (per ADC 1114 excerpt)". It is not a measured or planning transit time for a PD 02 CASREP part.
- **Spec 05 claim: `transit_days_conus` = 5 "default from ADC 1114" [S-SNIP]. PARTLY CONFIRMED.** The 5-day CONUS in-transit default exists [snippet] — [ADC 1114](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC_1114_Change_MRA_Times_based_on_TDD.pdf). But it is an MRA-computation default, and TP1 (PD 01-03) TDD standards are expected to be shorter (exact values not retrieved). `transit_days_oconus` 10 is [PROP] with no source.

### Inferences
- The scenario's PD 02 + RDD 999 CASREP from a CONUS ship that is not deploying within 30 days would fail a strict DoD edit (999 is restricted to overseas or deploying units). A Navy SME is likely to notice this.
- Because TP1 covers all PD 01-03, the spec's CASREP rows (PD 01-03) are TP1 regardless of RDD. The RDD-threshold logic only matters for PD 04-15 rows (the non-CASREP job parts).

### Gaps
- The current DoDM 4140.01 Vol 8 TDD segment tables (CONUS and OCONUS days per TP) were not retrieved. The LMI TDD page refused connection, and whs.mil and dla.mil were blocked.
- The current OPNAVINST 4614.1 revision text (H or later) and the current NAVSUP P-485 CASREP requisition rules were not retrieved.
- Current DLM 4000.25 Vol 2 Chapter 4 wording on 999 eligibility was not retrieved (only an Aug 2025 fragment surfaced).

## Q4. NSN/NIIN structure, formatting, and the fake-NIIN marker `01999xxxx`

### Takeaway
NSN = FSC(4) + NIIN(9), with NIIN = 2-digit NCB code + 7-digit item number, and the 4-2-3-4 dashed format: all **CONFIRMED**; 00/01 = United States. Real U.S. NIINs assigned in late 2024 to 2025 are around **01-72x-xxxx**, so `01-999-xxxx` is far above the active range and collision risk in 2026 is low. It is still inside the live U.S. block.

### Cited Findings
- **Spec claim (00 §4): "NSN FSC(4)-NIIN(9) [S]" and "NIIN = 2-digit NCB code + 7". CONFIRMED.** The NIIN is a two-digit National Codification Bureau (NCB) number followed by a seven-digit item number; "00" or "01" identify the United States [snippet] — [NATO NSN page](https://www.nato.int/structur/AC/135/nmcrl/data/nsn); [DLA Disposition Services NSNs](https://www.dla.mil/Disposition-Services/DDSR/Quick-Links/NSNs/)
- **Formatting `4320-01-999-0001`. CONFIRMED** as the standard 4-2-3-4 pattern. Real example: 7520-01-728-9777 — [ISO Group](https://www.iso-group.com/NSN/7520-01-728-9777)
- **Fake-NIIN marker `01999xxxx` [PH]. Low risk as of 2026, not collision-proof.**
