# MissionTwin spec accuracy check: identifiers (00), daily reports (16), MOVREP/LOGREQ (17), catalog and calibration (19), fielded access (20)

Checked on 4 Nov 2026 against public, unclassified sources. Verdict labels: **CONFIRMED**, **WRONG**, **OUTDATED**, **UNVERIFIABLE**. "[snippet]" means only a search-result excerpt was available because the primary document was blocked (HTTP 403 or a TLS certificate error on tpub subdomains). "[mirror]" means an unofficial copy of an official message was read. Older sources carry their dates. [PH] and [PROP] items are flagged only where they misrepresent the real format or process.

Highest-impact fixes, in order:
1. "DDG-99x, which no real ship uses" is false. DDG-993 was USS Kidd, and DD-991 and DD-992 were Spruance-class destroyers.
2. GAO-20-86's "about 70% late" covers **overseas-based** surface ships only.
3. GAO-12-887's "14.5" is an **average daily count of CASREPs per destroyer** (an inventory figure), not a volume of new reports.
4. The 84,379 vs 70,705 manning figures come from **GAO-24-106525**, not from the Military Times article.
5. The "700 applications and databases" figure describes the NOBLE legacy estate, not NTCSS alone.
6. The MOVREP "100 NM off track" trigger is misstated. The mock MOVREP's line format misrepresents the real one, and neither mock message has a precedence/DTG line.
7. The ADC 1114 "5-day CONUS transit" figure is unverified and probably a misreading.
8. "No. 2 SSDG" is wrong for a DDG-51.
9. In port, Eight O'Clock Reports go to the Command Duty Officer (CDO).

## 1. Shared identifiers and naming conventions (00_README sections 2-4)

### Takeaway
Most identifier formats hold up: the 5-character Navy UIC, the DTG form, Julian day 308, the 14-character MILSTRIP document number and "CNSL". The hull-number claim is wrong. DDG-993 was a real ship (USS Kidd), and DD-991 and DD-992 were real Spruance-class destroyers. The README should drop "no real ship uses" or switch to an unmistakably fictional hull scheme.

### Cited Findings
- **WRONG.** Spec claim: "Fictional hulls in the DDG-99x range, which no real ship uses. [PH]" Finding: USS Kidd was **DDG-993**, commissioned 27 Mar 1981 and decommissioned and stricken 12 Mar 1998. The Kidd class ran DDG-993 to DDG-996 (Kidd, Callaghan, Scott, Chandler) — [Wikipedia: USS Kidd (DDG-993)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Kidd_(DDG-993)); [GlobalSecurity DDG-993](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/systems/ship/ddg-993.htm). DD-991 (USS Fife, commissioned 31 May 1980) and DD-992 (USS Fletcher, 12 Jul 1980 to 1 Oct 2004) were Spruance-class destroyers — [Wikipedia: USS Fife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Fife); [NHHC DANFS: Fletcher (DD-992)](https://history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/f/fletcher-dd-992.html). DDG-991 and DDG-992 were never assigned with a DDG prefix, but DDG-993 was. **Fix:** change the sentence to "no ship in commission uses these numbers; DDG-993 was USS Kidd (1981-1998) and DD-991/992 were Spruance-class". Better, use an unmistakably fictional hull such as `DDG-X91`, `DDG-X92`, `DDG-X93` so no reviewer reads Ashby (DDG-993) as a nod to Kidd. Hull numbers DD-963 to DD-997 were all Spruance/Kidd, so every "99x" hull sits in historically used territory.
- **CONFIRMED (context).** Current Arleigh Burke hull numbers are nowhere near 99x. USS Harvey C. Barnum Jr. (DDG 124) was commissioned 11 Apr 2026, the future George M. Neal (DDG 131) was launched 1 Apr 2026, and the Navy has named DDG 146 (Robert Kerrey) — [Naval Technology](https://www.naval-technology.com/news/uss-harvey-barnum-commission/) [snippet]; [Army Recognition, 2026](https://armyrecognition.com/news/navy-news/2026/us-navy-launches-81st-arleigh-burke-class-destroyer-uss-george-m-neal-to-handle-multiple-attacks-at-once) [snippet]; [Shephard Media](https://www.shephardmedia.com/news/naval-warfare/us-navy-names-ddg-146-arleigh-burke-destroyer-the-uss-robert-kerrey/) [snippet, naming date not visible]. The Zumwalt class uses DDG-1000 to DDG-1002 (general knowledge, not re-sourced).
- **CONFIRMED.** Spec claim: "The UIC is 5 characters [S]." Navy UICs are five characters. The DoD-wide UIC is a six-character code. Navy usage puts "N" before the five digits (the archived Navy FOIA page says Navy contract numbers begin with "N" followed by the 5-digit UIC) — [Wikipedia: Unit Identification Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_Identification_Code); [Navy FOIA page, 2004 archive](https://webharvest.gov/peth04/20041014225207/http://foia.navy.mil/foia/commonlyrequested.asp) [snippet, DATED]. **Fix (clarity):** record both forms in the catalog (`uic: 99101`, `dod_uic: N99101`). DoD systems such as DRRS-S key on the 6-character form, while 3M JCNs use the 5-character form. That is an inference; DRRS-S UIC length was not checked.
- **UNVERIFIABLE.** Spec claim: "The DoDAAC form 'R + UIC' for afloat units is [UNV]." DLMS lists N, R and V (and Q for contractors, P for FMS) as Navy series. A DLM 4000.25 Vol 6 document calls N "the Navy standard for all Navy non-contractor DoDAACs". No source found ties "R" specifically to ships — [Wikipedia: DoDAAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense_Activity_Address_Code); [DLM 4000.25 Vol 6](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/Manuals/DLM/V6/0041-v6c2.docx) [snippet]. Keep it [UNV]. The DLA "DoDAAC Assignment Logic" PDF was blocked (403).
- **CONFIRMED.** Spec claim: CASREP DTG form `DDHHMMZ MON YY` (tagged "[UNV] exact form"). The DTG is day, time, zone letter, month and two-digit year, often written spaced as `DD HHMMZ MON YY` or run together — [Wikipedia: Date-time group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date-time_group). Real Navy messages use exactly this form in the header line, e.g. `R 241507Z JAN 20` — [NAVADMIN 017/20, mirror](https://navadmin-viewer.fly.dev/NAVADMIN/017/20). **Fix:** upgrade the tag from [UNV] to [S].
- **CONFIRMED.** Spec claim: "4 Nov 2026 = day 308, so YDDD = 6308." 2026 is not a leap year, and 4 Nov 2026 is day-of-year 308 (computed locally with .NET `DayOfYear`). So the MILSTRIP document number `R99101`+`6308`+`A001` = `R991016308A001` (14 characters) is internally consistent. The DoDAAC(6)+date(4)+serial(4) structure itself was not re-sourced this session.
- **CONFIRMED.** Spec claim: TYCOM `CNSL` ("code style [UNV]"). CNSL is the official short form for Commander, Naval Surface Force Atlantic. SURFLANT's own site has a "Contact CNSL" page, and DVIDS captioned the May 2026 change of command "CNSL change of command ceremony" — [surflant.usff.navy.mil Contact-CNSL](https://www.surflant.usff.navy.mil/Contact-CNSL/); [DVIDS, May 2026](https://www.dvidshub.net/image/9683250/cnsl-change-command-ceremony). COMNAVSURFLANT is the surface TYCOM under USFF, headquartered at Naval Station Norfolk — [Wikipedia: COMNAVSURFLANT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander,_Naval_Surface_Force_Atlantic). **Fix:** upgrade the tag to [S].
- **CONFIRMED (internal check).** The scenario clock and DTG examples in Zulu match Navy practice. MOVREP "times in movement reports shall be in ZULU" — [SWOS 72-7, REV 2/96](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html) [DATED].

### Inferences
- Because any 99x DDG/DD hull overlaps 1970s-80s ships, a judge or Navy mentor will probably notice "USS Ashby (DDG-993)" sits on USS Kidd's hull number. Using an alphanumeric hull removes the issue and makes synthetic records easy to grep.
- `DESRON 99` / `COMDESRON NINE NINE` follow real naming style (numbers spelled out in plain-language addresses), but this session found no public PLA list to confirm the spelling convention.

### Gaps
- No authoritative public source ties the DoDAAC "R" series to afloat units. The DLA assignment-logic PDF was blocked.
- No way to check whether the placeholder UIC `99101` or NIIN block `01-999-xxxx` collides with a real assignment. The SNDL and FLIS were not accessible.
- CASREP serial (001-999) and priority designator (01-15) were not re-checked this session; they belong to the CASREP and MILSTRIP specs.

## 2. Navy record-message format in the mock messages (00 section 2 banner rule; 17 templates)

### Takeaway
The mocks get the skeleton right: FM/TO, BT, an inner classification line, a MSGID set and slash-slash set terminators. Both mocks omit the precedence + DTG line that real messages carry, even though MissionTwin keys messages on the DTG. The LOGREQ mock also omits the inner classification line.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED.** Real Navy record messages use this layout:
  ```
  UNCLASSIFIED//
  ROUTINE
  R 241507Z JAN 20 MID...
  FM CNO WASHINGTON DC
  TO NAVADMIN
  INFO CNO WASHINGTON DC
  BT
  UNCLAS
  NAVADMIN 017/20
  MSGID/GENADMIN/CNO WASHINGTON DC/N2N6G/JAN//
  SUBJ/...//
  ```
  The message ends with `BT`, `#0001`, `NNNN` — [NAVADMIN 017/20, mirror](https://navadmin-viewer.fly.dev/NAVADMIN/017/20). NAVADMIN 315/18 shows the same pattern (`R 261805Z DEC 18`, `FM CNO WASHINGTON DC`, `TO NAVADMIN`, `BT`, `UNCLAS`, `MSGID/GENADMIN/CNO WASHINGTON DC/N2N6/DEC//`) — [NAVADMIN 315/18, mirror](https://navadmin-viewer.fly.dev/NAVADMIN/315/18). The official mynavyhr `.txt` copies returned 403.
- **WRONG (format omission).** The MOVREP and LOGREQ templates go straight from the banner to `FM`, with no precedence/DTG line (`R 081200Z NOV 26`). Real messages carry the DTG in that line — [NAVADMIN 017/20, mirror](https://navadmin-viewer.fly.dev/NAVADMIN/017/20). The README says DTGs are the message key, and the files are named `<ship_id>_<dtg>.txt`. **Fix:** add a `R ddhhmmZ MON YY` line (precedence `R` routine; SWOS says MOVREP arrival reports use `P` priority) after the banner in both templates, and have the parser read the DTG from that line rather than the filename.
- **CONFIRMED.** The MSGID set structure `MSGID/<message type>/<originator>/<serial or office code>/<month>//` matches real messages (`MSGID/GENADMIN/CNO WASHINGTON DC/N2N6/DEC//`) — [NAVADMIN 315/18, mirror](https://navadmin-viewer.fly.dev/NAVADMIN/315/18). For MOVREPs, "the message identification line must be used first" — [SWOS 72-7](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html) [DATED 1996]. The mock `MSGID/MOVREP/USS ASHBY/004/NOV//` fits.
- **WRONG (inconsistency).** The LOGREQ template has no inner classification line after `BT`, while the MOVREP template has `UNCLAS //N03120//` and real messages have `UNCLAS` — [NAVADMIN 017/20, mirror](https://navadmin-viewer.fly.dev/NAVADMIN/017/20). **Fix:** add `UNCLAS` after `BT` in the LOGREQ template.
- **UNVERIFIABLE.** The SSIC `//N03120//` on the MOVREP mock. The two NAVADMINs checked show bare `UNCLAS` with no SSIC. A tpub meteorology training page that turned up in MOVREP searches is headed `UNCLAS//N03123//` — [tpub meteorology training](https://meteorologytraining.tpub.com/14269/css/14269_182.htm) [snippet; page blocked by certificate error]. **Fix:** drop the SSIC or tag it [PROP]. Do not present N03120 as the MOVREP SSIC.
- **CONFIRMED (CONFIDENTIAL in some cases).** MOVREP classification is "normally UNCLAS in peacetime" but CONFIDENTIAL "when reporting movements that need diplomatic clearance or movements of a task organization" — [SWOS 72-7](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html) [DATED 1996]. The UNCLAS single-ship VACAPES-to-Norfolk mock is plausible.
- **CONTEXT (no spec claim).** The Navy moved shore organizational messaging from DMS to NREMS, which uses an AMHS back end, starting about 2007 — [Wikipedia: AMHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_Message_Handling_System); [Wikipedia: Defense Message System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Message_System) [secondary, DATED]. The specs name no transport system, so nothing needs correcting.

### Inferences
- Adding the precedence/DTG line makes the mock files parse the way real traffic would, through the header rather than a filename convention. That is a credibility point if a Navy mentor reads the raw `.txt`.
- The `UNCLAS//SYNTHETIC EXERCISE//` banner works as a [PROP] variant of the real `UNCLASSIFIED//` top line, as long as the inner `UNCLAS` line is kept.

### Gaps
- No 2020s public source confirms the Navy's current record-message transport (NREMS/AMHS or successor), or whether `BT`/`NNNN` survive in all current traffic. They do appear in 2018 and 2020 NAVADMIN text.
- No public MOVREP addressing guidance (action and info addressees) was found.

## 3. Eight O'Clock Reports, Plan of the Day and SOE (spec 16)

### Takeaway
The spec's description of Eight O'Clock Reports and the POD is accurate. Two template details are wrong for this scenario. In port, Eight O'Clock Reports go to the Command Duty Officer, not XO to CO, and Kessler is in port. DDG-51s have gas turbine generators, not SSDGs. The 2000-local = 0100Z timing is correct for the scenario dates.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED [snippet].** Spec claim: Eight O'Clock Reports are "daily equipment status reports" to the CO through the XO, "usually locally generated forms." tpub ET text: "daily equipment status reports given to the commanding officer by the executive officer each evening at 8:00 p.m. (2000)" — [tpub ET, Eight O'Clock Reports](https://electronicstechnician.tpub.com/14085/css/Eight-Oclock-Reports-22.htm) [snippet; fetch blocked by certificate error].
- **WRONG for the scenario (needs in-port variant).** Underway, the CSO/weapons officer gives the combat systems Eight O'Clock Reports to the XO. **In port, the duty department officers give them to the Command Duty Officer (CDO).** Contents are major weapons systems status, estimated time of repair, parts ordering status and daily system operability test results. They were traditionally verbal, often backed by a written checklist or master sheet — [tpub FC, "Eight and Twelve O'Clock" reports](https://firecontrolman.tpub.com/14098/css/Work-Center-Pass-Down-Log-12.htm) [snippet]; [tpub FC vol. 1](https://tpub.com/fcv1/2.htm) [snippet]. Kessler is "IN PORT NORFOLK PIER 12 THROUGH 121200Z NOV 26" in spec 17, but the template header reads "From: Executive Officer To: Commanding Officer". **Fix:** for in-port days, use "To: Command Duty Officer (for CO)" with entries from duty department heads, or keep XO-to-CO and tag the header [PROP] "written consolidation of the in-port 2000 reports".
- **WRONG (template realism).** "ENGINEERING ... No. 2 SSDG lube oil leak." The DDG-51 class has no ship service diesel generators. It has **three ship service gas turbine generator sets** (Allison 501-K34, now Rolls-Royce AG9140; AG9160RF on Flight III), each about 2.5 MW on 450 V, 60 Hz — [FAS DDG-51](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/sys/ship/ddg-51.htm) [snippet]; [Rolls-Royce AG9140](https://www.rolls-royce.com/products-and-services/defence/naval/gas-turbines/ag9140-generator-set.aspx) [snippet]; [tpub Electric Plant, 501-K34](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14115/Electric-Plant-211.html) [snippet]. **Fix:** change to "No. 2 SSGTG lube oil leak". "All GTMs up" is fine (LM2500 main engines).
- **CONFIRMED (timing).** Spec claim: "As of" set to "the next day at 0100Z (2000 local)". US DST ended Sunday 1 Nov 2026, so Norfolk is on EST (UTC-5) from then on. 2000 EST on 5 Nov 2026 = 06 Nov 0100Z (computed locally with the Windows Eastern time-zone rules). Caveat: any report dated 31 Oct or earlier would be 0000Z (EDT, UTC-4). The earliest generated report (day -3 = 1 Nov) falls after the 0200 changeover, so the rule holds for all 6 days.
- **CONFIRMED (secondary).** Spec claim: "Plan of the Day (POD): issued by the XO; sets the next day's sequence of events." The POD is "the proposed plan for the unit for that particular day ... prepared in advance by the ship's Yeoman. The issuer is the ship's executive officer" — [The Lean Submariner, 2022](https://theleansubmariner.com/2022/01/18/holiday-routine-u-s-s-vestal-daily-employment-schedule-for-sunday-7-december-1941/). An XO billet description includes "Ensure Plan-of-the-Day is prepared and promulgated" — [NOAA Corps XO billet](https://www.corpscpc.noaa.gov/careermgmt/pdf/billets/0731.pdf) [snippet; NOAA analog]. The SORM (OPNAVINST 3120.32D) POD wording was not retrieved.
- **UNVERIFIABLE.** Spec claim: an SOE "has event, time, description and remarks columns with spaced event numbers [DATED]". No source found this session. Keep [DATED]/[PROP].
- **CONFIRMED [DATED].** Spec 16 section 7 cites SWOS 72-9 for the OPSUM. SWOS lesson 72.9 (REV 07/95) describes a daily OPSUM "each day that the ship is underway, and inport during deployment in some cases" — [SWOS 72-9](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-9.html).

### Inferences
- The spec's design point holds: there is no system of record, and formats are local and largely free text or verbal. That supports the LLM-extraction path.
- Internal check: the template line "single array face ops since 0412 on the 4th" does not match the CASREP DTG `041200Z NOV 26` (0700 EST). Either use "since 0700" (local) or reference "041200Z". Otherwise the extractor oracle may see a contradiction that was not intended.
- Minor realism: DDG department heads are usually LTs. "LCDR Osei" as Chief Engineer is possible but less typical (general knowledge, not sourced).

### Gaps
- The SORM (OPNAVINST 3120.32D) text on the POD and Eight O'Clock Reports was not retrieved.
- No public source on SOE column layout or event-number spacing.

## 4. MOVREP, LOGREQ and OPSUM (spec 17)

### Takeaway
MOVREP timing and the 4-hour ETA trigger are right. The "100 NM off track" trigger is misstated: 100 NM applies to MODLOC or carrier operations, and track deviation uses 4 hours. The mock's free-text "1. 2. 3." lines misrepresent the two-digit numbered, END-terminated format. LOGREQ's purpose is confirmed, but no Navy-wide lead time exists publicly, and a LOGREQ for the ship's own homeport pier is an unusual use.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED [DATED 1996].** Spec claim: MOVREP "sent 48-24 hours before departure." SWOS lesson 72.7: "Departure reports should be submitted 48 to 24 hours prior to departure" — [SWOS 72-7, REV 2/96](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html).
- **CONFIRMED [DATED].** Spec claim: updates "triggered by a 4-hour ETA change". SWOS: "There is a variation of more than 4 hours in the estimated time of arrival (ETA)". It also lists "a variation of more than four (4) hours between the estimated and actual times of departure" — [SWOS 72-7](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html).
- **WRONG.** Spec claim: "or being 100 NM off track". SWOS gives the track-position trigger as "a variation of four (4) hours (100NM for carrier operations)" from the intended track. Separately, "a variation of more than 100NM from a reference point of operations (MODLOC)" triggers a report. Other triggers: joining or division of units, changes in composition, storm evasion (report at least every 24 hours, plus on completion), OTSR routing changes, and medical officer arrival or departure — [SWOS 72-7](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html). **Fix:** "updates when ETD or ETA varies by more than 4 hours, when the ship is more than 4 hours off its intended track (100 NM for carrier ops), or more than 100 NM from a MODLOC reference point; plus joins, splits, storm evasion and OTSR changes."
- **CONFIRMED [DATED].** Spec claim: "cancellation sent if the ship does not sail on time." SWOS: a CHG CNL report is used "only when a ship does not get underway on time", and a new sail plan can go in the same message — [SWOS 72-7](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html).
- **OMISSION.** "Arrival reports must be submitted immediately upon arrival with a priority precedence" — [SWOS 72-7](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html). **Fix:** the generator rule "one MOVREP per transit" should be departure MOVREP 24-48 hours before ETD plus an arrival MOVREP at arrival time. The Ashby day-5 cross-deck availability should key off the arrival report, not the departure report.
- **WRONG (mock format misrepresents the real one).** Spec claim: "numbered lines in Zulu time" is right, but the template uses `1. DEPART ...` free-text sentences. SWOS: "Each line in a report shall be given a sequential two-digit number. The first line in the report is numbered 01"; "All times in movement reports shall be in ZULU"; the last line ends by "writing END after the double slash"; the message ID line comes first; an ORG line is "not required in a movement report for a single ship proceeding independently" — [SWOS 72-7](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html). **Fix:** number lines `01`, `02`, ..., start with `01 MSGID/MOVREP/USS ASHBY/004/NOV//`, and end the last set with `//END`. Keep the data set contents tagged [PROP], since current set names were not retrieved.
- **OUTDATED citation.** Spec cites only SWOS 72-7 (1996), which references "NWP 10-1-10, Operational Reports" — [SWOS 72-7](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html). A Navy training page says MOVREPs are discussed in **NWP 1-03.1** — [tpub meteorology training](https://meteorologytraining.tpub.com/14272/css/14272_33.htm) [snippet]. The MOVREP system collects "vital information" on the status, location and movement of flag commands, commissioned fleet units and ships under operational control, under Navy and Coast Guard control — [Wikipedia: Movement Report System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_Report_System). **Fix:** name NWP 1-03.1 (Operational Reports; successor to NWP 10-1-10) as the governing pub, current edition and NWP-versus-NTTP designation unverified. Note that 00_README calls the CASREP pub "NTTP 1-03.1", and this designation conflict is unresolved.
- **UNVERIFIABLE.** Spec 17 template addresses the MOVREP `TO COMDESRON NINE NINE`. No public addressing rules were found; real MOVREPs feed the movement-report control system. **Fix:** tag the addressees [PROP] and consider adding an `INFO` line.
- **CONFIRMED (secondary).** Spec claim: LOGREQ "requests port and at-sea services through NAVSUP Fleet Logistics Centers [S]". Ships send a logistics requisition message (LOGREQ) "in advance of any port visit, or for delivery at sea". FLCs route items: food through DLA Troop Support, fuel through DLA Energy, parts through FLC staff — [Maritime Professional](https://www.maritimeprofessional.com/news/delivering-parts-fuel-mail-over-347860). A 2011 USS Eisenhower LOGREQ covered port operations support, trash removal, crane and vehicle rentals, utility hookups and food — [GlobalSecurity/NNS, Aug 2011](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2011/08/mil-110802-nns09.htm) [DATED]. NAVSUP FLC Norfolk describes the flow: the ship identifies services, then the command requests a quote from the port's husbanding service provider and issues an order — [navy.mil, FLC Norfolk Fleet Week](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/Press-Releases/display-pressreleases/Article/2257152/navsup-flc-norfolk-supports-ships-for-fleet-week-new-york/). The NAVSUP FLC Pearl Harbor LSC page cited by the spec returned 403 this session.
- **UNVERIFIABLE.** LOGREQ lead time (the brief asked about "72-hour LOGREQ?"). No Navy-wide "days prior" standard was found. The Coast Guard (Base New Orleans) requires arriving cutters to submit a LOGREQ "not less than 7 days in advance of arrival" and to receive a reply "no later than 48 hours prior to requested arrival" — [USCG Base New Orleans](https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Our-Organization/Operational-Logistics-Command-LOGCOM/Bases/Base-New-Orleans/Comptroller-Base-Operations/Logistics-Requirements/). An NPS thesis analyses the Navy LOGREQ process — [NPS, "An analysis of the Logistics Requisition process"](https://calhoun.nps.edu/entities/publication/b65dcb9b-8d2d-44e1-9e18-a14f1f63ee6b) [not read]. **Fix:** if the generator needs a lead time, use "LOGREQ DTG at least 7 days before arrival, updates as needed [PROP, informed by USCG practice]". Do not cite 72 hours.
- **WRONG (misrepresents use).** The Kessler LOGREQ mock requests Norfolk pier delivery and services while the ship sits in its own homeport ("1. ARRIVAL: IN PORT NORFOLK PIER 12 THROUGH ..."). The sources describe LOGREQs for port visits and at-sea delivery — [Maritime Professional](https://www.maritimeprofessional.com/news/delivering-parts-fuel-mail-over-347860). **Fix:** do one of three things. (a) Model the delivery window as a port visit away from homeport, or as an at-sea delivery (VERTREP/COD) before Mission A. (b) Keep the in-port window but source it from the schedule (`schedule_30day.csv`) plus requisition "deliver to pier" data. (c) Tag the homeport LOGREQ explicitly [PROP] as a simplification. In any LOGREQ, list CASREP material by **MILSTRIP document number** (`R991016308A001`) as well as NIIN, use an ETA/ETD line instead of "ARRIVAL: IN PORT ... THROUGH", and add the inner `UNCLAS` line (see section 2).
- **CONFIRMED [DATED 1995].** Spec claim: OPSUM is a "daily operational summary following an OPGEN base format that commanders modify". SWOS 72.9 (REV 07/95): "The basic format for the OPSUM is found in the NAVY WIDE OPGEN"; "Any modifications made by the operational commander will be found in the Supplemental OPGEN"; sent each day underway, and in port during deployment in some cases — [SWOS 72-9](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-9.html). The same lesson notes OPAREA/service requests are typically submitted 14 days before the evolution.

### Inferences
- With the corrected triggers, a MissionTwin donor-proximity rule should treat a MOVREP position as stale only after an ETA change of more than 4 hours or a new MOVREP. It should not use a 100 NM deviation for a DDG proceeding independently.
- Because LOGREQs are tied to port visits and at-sea delivery, the stronger demo story for "when can the part physically arrive" is an at-sea or port-visit delivery window, since Kessler would otherwise just receive the part at its homeport pier.

### Gaps

## 9. Classification by compilation: GAO-12-887, DRRS and the OPNAVINST 5513.3 series (spec 20 section 5)

### Takeaway
GAO-12-887 does say individual-ship CASREPs, DRRS-N data and INSURV reports are classified when they identify a ship's warfighting capabilities, and that above-ship summaries are unclassified. The spec's "generally CUI" half is not from GAO. DRRS up to SECRET is confirmed. OPNAVINST 5513.3C exists, but it is a 2008 index of surface warfare SCGs, not the CASREP SCG itself.

### Cited Findings
- **CONFIRMED (second half) / UNVERIFIABLE (first half).** Spec claim: "Single-ship CASREP, maintenance and supply records are generally CUI; individual-ship CASREPs become classified when they reveal warfighting capability [S] (GAO-12-887)." GAO: "Casualty reports, DRRS-N data, and INSURV reports are all classified when they identify warfighting capabilities of individual ships". When CASREP and INSURV information is summarized above the individual-ship level, it is unclassified — [GAO-12-887](https://www.gao.gov/assets/650/648722.txt). GAO says nothing about records being "generally CUI". **Fix:** split the sentence so that "[S] GAO-12-887" covers only the classified-when-revealing-capability rule (which also applies to DRRS-N and INSURV), and tag "generally CUI" [UNV].
- **CONFIRMED [DATED 2020].** Spec claim: "DRRS handles data up to SECRET". USFFINST 3501.6 (14 Jun 2020) says DRRS can process, store and display information classified up to SECRET. DRRS-S is a SIPRNet-accessible web application, and since 1 Oct 2019 OSD-hosted DRRS-S has been the Navy's primary readiness input tool — [USFFINST 3501.6](https://elearning.sabresystems.com/drrsS/instructions/USFFINST%203501_6%2014June2020.pdf) [snippet]. No newer revision of 3501.6 was found.
- **CONFIRMED, but it is an index.** Spec claim: "engage the security classification guide owner early (OPNAVINST 5513.3C cited, not retrieved)". OPNAVINST 5513.3C (21 Jul 2008) is the DON list of security classification guides for **surface warfare programs**, listing active, new, revised and cancelled SCGs in enclosures (1)-(4) — [GlobalSpec](https://standards.globalspec.com/std/1104959/opnav-5513-3c) [snippet]; [FAS index of OPNAVINST 5513 series](https://irp.fas.org/doddir/navy/opnavinst/scg.html). OPNAVINST 5513.1F requires OCAs to prepare an SCG for each system, plan or program — [OPNAVINST 5513.1F, archived](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130219013229/https://doni.daps.dla.mil/Directives/05000%20General%20Management%20Security%20and%20Safety%20Services/05-500%20Security%20Services/5513.1F.pdf) [snippet]. **Fix:** "OPNAVINST 5513.3C (2008) indexes the surface warfare SCGs; the CASREP/readiness SCG and its OCA must be identified from that index or its successor; currency of the 3C revision unconfirmed".
- **CONFIRMED (supporting).** GAO reports DRRS-N data as classified, "normally reported monthly" or "within 24 hours of a significant change" — [GAO-12-887](https://www.gao.gov/assets/650/648722.txt) [DATED 2012].
- **CONFIRMED (supporting).** MOVREPs can themselves be CONFIDENTIAL when reporting task-organization movements or movements needing diplomatic clearance — [SWOS 72-7](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html) [DATED 1996]. Joining squadron MOVREPs with readiness data could therefore raise classification independently of CASREPs.

### Inferences
- GAO's "summarized above the individual ship level ... unclassified" does not cover MissionTwin's fleet map, because that map names individual ships and forecasts their mission losses. GAO's logic, which keys on identifying individual ships' warfighting capabilities, supports the spec's view that the fleet-wide forward map looks like SECRET readiness output. Even the single-ship forecast may be classified, so the "IL5 for unit and squadron views" plan should be validated with the SCG owner rather than assumed.
- Jupiter's availability on SIPR (section 6) gives a plausible accredited home for the classified aggregate view.

### Gaps
- The text of the CASREP/readiness SCG, and the current revision of the 5513.3 series, are not public.
- No public rule says whether a forward-looking projection of Y/Q/N ratings inherits DRRS classification.
