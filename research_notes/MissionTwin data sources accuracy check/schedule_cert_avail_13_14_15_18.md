# Fact-check of MissionTwin specs 13 (OFRP schedule), 14 (certifications/events), 15 (availabilities/NMD) and 18 (INSURV) against public sources, as of 4 Nov 2026

Method note: claims are quoted from the spec, then labelled **CONFIRMED / WRONG / OUTDATED / UNVERIFIABLE**. Many primary Navy hosts (navy.mil, cpf.navy.mil, allhands.navy.mil, navsea.navy.mil, insurv.usff.navy.mil, news.usni.org, usni.org, cbo.gov, secnav.navy.mil) returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool. Where only a search-result excerpt was available, the citation says "(snippet)". Pages actually fetched and read in full: DVIDS (Bulkeley 2021, early-certification 2019, Gonzalez Group Sail 2026), gao.gov product pages (GAO-20-86, GAO-25-106990, GAO-12-887, GAO-23-105341), Defense One (Jan 2026), Navy Times (29 Oct 2026), SWOS 72-2 (FAS mirror).

## Q1. OFRP employment schedule (spec 13): instructions, phases, cycle length, scheduling process, employment codes, scheduling IT system

### Takeaway
The instruction numbers, the four phase names and the 36-month notional cycle are correct. Three things need fixing. `DEPLOYED` is not an OFRP phase: deployment sits inside the Sustainment phase. The fleet scheduling tool *is* publicly named: **WebSked**, the CNO-approved employment scheduling tool, originally a GCCS-M module. Every ship-level process detail and employment code comes from a 1998 SWOS lesson and must stay tagged [DATED]. The 2026 operating context (300+ day deployments, maintenance "snowball") also undercuts the spec's claim that a 2-4 week schedule is "mostly fixed."

### Cited Findings
**Instructions**
- **CONFIRMED (number), UNVERIFIABLE (still current).** Spec: "OPNAVINST 3000.15A". DONI lists OPNAVINST 3000.15A, USFF/CNO N3/N5, dated 10 Nov 2014, applying to "all active and reserve deployable units". No OPNAVINST 3000.15B was found. — [DONI 3000.15A](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/doni/Directives/03000%20Naval%20Operations%20and%20Readiness/03-00%20General%20Operations%20and%20Readiness%20Support/3000.15A.pdf) (snippet; direct fetch rejected)
- **CONFIRMED, add detail.** Spec: "COMUSFLTFORCOM/COMPACFLTINST 3000.15B (Oct 2020)". The document is "COMUSFLTFORCOM/COMPACFLTINST 3000.15B and COMUSNAVEUR/COMUSNAVAFINST 3000.15, 20 Oct 2020". It says OFRP "is the force readiness administrative control (ADCON) of" USFF, CPF and NAVEUR/NAVAF. — [OFRP instruction copy](https://elearning.sabresystems.com/drrsS/instructions/OPTIMIZED%20FLEET%20RESPONSE%20PLAN.pdf) (snippet; host unreachable on fetch). It is also cited by [CUSFFC/CPF 3501.3E (Apr 2021)](https://elearning.sabresystems.com/drrsS/instructions/CUSFFC_CPF%203501.3E_FTC_Apr2021.pdf) and [BUMEDINST 3000.1](https://www.med.navy.mil/Portals/62/Documents/BUMED/Directives/Instructions/3000.1.pdf). The predecessor fleet instruction was 3000.15A of 8 Dec 2014 — [GAO-16-466R](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-16-466r.pdf) (snippet). No 3000.15C was found. **Correction:** write "COMUSFLTFORCOM/COMPACFLTINST 3000.15B / COMUSNAVEUR/COMUSNAVAFINST 3000.15 (20 Oct 2020)", and cite it directly instead of through BUMEDINST 3000.1.

**Phases and cycle**
- **CONFIRMED.** Spec: phases "maintenance, basic, integrated/advanced, sustainment". The 2018 SFTRM says OFRP has four phases: "Maintenance, Basic, Advanced/Integrated, and Sustainment" — [COMNAVSURFPAC/COMNAVSURFLANTINST 3502.7 (1 Nov 2018)](https://allhands.navy.mil/Portals/1/Optask/references/COMNAVSURFPAC_COMNAVSURFLANTINST-3502_7-1Nov18-Surface_Force_Training_Readiness_Manual.pdf?ver=W4gxIOXq_F2u55iCVjiVzQ%3D%3D) (snippet)
- **WRONG (misrepresents the phase vocabulary).** Spec `ofrp_phase` enum: "`MAINTENANCE`, `BASIC`, `INTEGRATED`, `SUSTAINMENT`, `DEPLOYED`". Deployment is not its own phase. Navy Times, citing GAO, describes the 36-month cycle as "eight months to depot-level maintenance, 10 months to training" and 18 months "to deployment and sustainment" — [Navy Times, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.navytimes.com/news/your-navy/2026/10/29/record-deployment-for-destroyer-uss-spruance-casts-doubt-over-us-navy-maintenance/). **Correction:** drop `DEPLOYED` from `ofrp_phase`. Use `ADVANCED_INTEGRATED` (or keep `INTEGRATED`, noting the SFTRM name). Add a separate boolean `deployed`, or keep deployment as an employment value inside `SUSTAINMENT`.
- **CONFIRMED [S-SNIP].** Spec: "Notional 36 months; 'often ends up clocking in at 42 to 44 months'". The USNI News excerpt says OFRP is meant to produce a 36-month cycle for carriers and surface ships with a seven-month deployment, but it often runs 42 to 44 months — [USNI News, 12 Feb 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/02/12/improving-maintenance-timelines-key-to-on-time-strike-group-deployments-cno-says) (snippet; 403 on fetch). An earlier report put the average at 45 months against the 36-month target — [Defense News, Jul 2022](https://www.defensenews.com/naval/2022/07/12/navys-atlantic-fleet-mulling-new-high-readiness-force/) (snippet). The spec's "CSG-focused" caveat is fair: the excerpt names carriers and surface ships.
- **New context, Nov 2026 (supports adding a caveat).** USS Spruance had been deployed 342 days as of 29 Oct 2026, against the "seven-month deployment benchmark". On 26 Oct, CNO Adm. Daryl Caudle warned maintenance could "snowball" as ships return from 300+ day deployments and called it "a years-long recovery" — [Navy Times, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.navytimes.com/news/your-navy/2026/10/29/record-deployment-for-destroyer-uss-spruance-casts-doubt-over-us-navy-maintenance/). A USNI piece is headlined "Long Deployments to Be Navy Standard For Next Couple Years, MCPON, CNO Say". Per the excerpt, deployments past 11 months followed the late-February 2026 strikes on Iran — [USNI News](https://news.usni.org/?p=126283) (snippet). The same Navy Times article shows how far a schedule can move: Spruance's restricted availability was Jul 3 2026-Feb 21 2027 in the FY2025 budget, and Jan 19-Apr 28 2027 in the FY2027 budget.

**Scheduling process**
- **CONFIRMED [DATED, REV 03/98].** Spec: "Operations Officer owns the ship's schedule". SWOS 72-2 says: "The ship's Operations Officer is primarily responsible for the ship's schedule". The CO must approve the plan before it leaves the ship — [SWOS 72-2](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-2.html)
- **CONFIRMED [DATED].** Spec: "Long-range schedule rolls 18-24 months ahead, updated quarterly". SWOS 72-2 says to plan "at least 18-24 months in advance (6-8 quarters)" as a rolling continuum. That plan feeds the "quarterly employment schedule conference" (reference: CINCLANT/PACFLT NOTICE C3120, Quarterly Employment Schedule, "EMPSKED") — [SWOS 72-2](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-2.html)
- **UNVERIFIABLE.** Spec: "Quarterly scheduling conference locks underway inputs ~6 months ahead". This was not found in SWOS 72-2. It may be in SWOS 72-3 or 72-4, which were not read. **Correction:** tag [UNV] unless 72-3 or 72-4 confirms it.
- **CONFIRMED (as cited).** Spec: "Fleet Forces quarterly schedule review" (TYCOM scheduler posting). The original link was not re-fetched. The spec's [S] tag rests on a job posting, so it is weak evidence.
- **UNVERIFIABLE.** Spec: "For a 2-4 week horizon the schedule is mostly fixed". No source was found. The 2026 Spruance and Iran-deployment reporting above suggests schedules are unusually volatile. **Correction:** tag [PROP] and treat it as an assumption in `delay_model_params.yaml`.

**Employment codes**
- **CONFIRMED [DATED].** Spec: "RFS (ready for sea), UPK (upkeep), HOLUPK (holiday leave and upkeep)". SWOS 72-2 defines them as follows:
  - **RFS**: "Scheduled after an inport period longer than two months, or before a major underway period".
  - **UPK**: "Scheduled after major repair/maintenance events or major at-sea operations".
  - **HOLUPK**: "Scheduled during the Christmas/Chanukah and New Year holidays".
  - It also defines a TBD code for open schedule space.
  - Source: [SWOS 72-2](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-2.html)
- **Partly sourced [DATED]: other employment/availability terms.** **TAV** (Technical Availability) is "an availability for the accomplishment of specific items of work by a repair activity, during which the ship's ability to fully perform its assigned mission and tasks is not affected". **RAV** (Restricted Availability) is assigned by TYCOMs, and the ship cannot fully perform its missions during it. Tender-assisted upkeep was called "tender availability". — [NAVEDTRA FC/ET manuals mirrored on tpub](https://firecontrolman.tpub.com/14097/css/Shipyard-Overhaul-31.htm); [tpub ET](https://electronicstechnician.tpub.com/14084/css/Postoverhaul-Inspection-38.htm). Both are 1990s-era training manuals.
- **UNVERIFIABLE:** **LOCOP** (local operations), **ISE** (independent steaming exercise) and **IPT** were not defined in any source found. ISE appears only in passing in [SWOS Gunnery Officer INFO5](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/gunno/INFO5.html). **Correction:** keep the invented `UNDERWAY`, `INPORT`, `AVAIL` values tagged [PROP]. Optionally add `TAV` and `RAV` ([DATED]) and `LOCOP` ([UNV]).
- **Demo realism (RFS).** SWOS 72-2 places RFS before a major underway period. Kessler goes from `UPK` (day 0-8) straight into Group Sail on day 9. A short `RFS` block before day 9 would match the sourced vocabulary — [SWOS 72-2](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-2.html)

**Scheduling IT system**
- **WRONG.** Spec: "No public source names the current fleet scheduling IT system or its export format". The tool is publicly named **WebSked**:
  - A 2002 report calls WebSked "the newest module of the Global Command and Control System-Maritime (GCCS-M)", built by SPAWAR Systems Center San Diego with FGM Inc. on Lotus Domino. Atlantic Fleet was to deploy it in June 2002 and Pacific Fleet later that summer — [Route Fifty, Apr 2002](https://route-fifty.com/cybersecurity/2002/04/navy-adopts-web-app-to-schedule-ships/285788) (snippet).
  - A 2004 SPAWAR paper says it schedules about 99% of Navy units/ships and replaced a legacy desktop system. It also names a predecessor, VIPER. — [WebSked, 2004 CCRTS paper](http://www.dodccrp.org/events/2004_CCRTS/CD/papers/115.pdf) (snippet).
  - A USFF job posting describes managing "the CNO approved Employment Scheduling software tool (WebSked)" — [USFF fleet operations scheduling specialist posting](https://ability.usnlx.com/norfolk-va/fleet-operations-scheduling-specialist/F5522A489A80484783BE16A776EB324C/job); see also [ClearanceJobs fleet scheduler](https://www.clearancejobs.com/jobs/8966899/fleet-scheduler) (snippet; posting date and exact host unverified).
  - **Correction:** "Fleet employment schedules are maintained in WebSked, the CNO-approved Employment Scheduling tool (GCCS-M heritage) [S-SNIP]. Its export format is not public [UNV]." The term "Quarterly Employment Schedule (EMPSKED)" is also sourced [DATED].

### Inferences
- The `schedule_30day.csv` grain (blocks with one employment each) fits the "employment schedule" concept. Real employment schedules are promulgated quarterly — [WebSked 2004 paper](http://www.dodccrp.org/events/2004_CCRTS/CD/papers/115.pdf) (snippet). A `status` of TENTATIVE vs FIRM is a reasonable [PROP] stand-in for draft quarterly inputs vs the promulgated schedule.
- Kessler is in the Integrated phase with Group Sail on day 9 and COMPTUEX on day 21. Group Sail preceding COMPTUEX is confirmed (see Q2). The short six-day gap between them was not verified against any real CSG timeline.

### Gaps
- Full text of 3000.15B: phase-duration tables, cycle lengths for amphibious, FDNF and independent-deployer DDGs. Every host carrying it was blocked or unreachable.
- Whether an OPNAVINST 3000.15B exists, or whether 3000.15A is still the CNO-level version, in Nov 2026.
- A current, authoritative employment-code list (WebSked code table). None is public.
- Whether WebSked is still the system in 2026, or has been migrated. Only an undated job posting suggests continued use.

## Q2. Certifications and training events (spec 14): SFTRM edition, READ-E, Basic Phase certifications, Group Sail/SWATT/COMPTUEX, periodicity and lapse, ATG role, early certification

### Takeaway
The READ-E 4 description, the Curtis Wilbur and McCain anecdotes and the Group Sail→COMPTUEX order are all correct, but they come from 2018-2021 and should be tagged [DATED]. "Ships can **now** certify early" is a 2018/2019 SFTRM change, not a 2024-2026 one. Certification lapse is better supported by GAO's 2017 testimony (37% of Japan-based CG/DDG warfare certifications expired) than by an inference from a 2026 Proceedings article. The only publicly retrievable SFTRM is COMNAVSURFPAC/COMNAVSURFLANTINST 3502.7 of 1 Nov 2018. Its current revision is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
**SFTRM edition**
- **UNVERIFIABLE (current edition).** README §9: "Current SFTRM (3502.7 series) certification list". The public copy is COMNAVSURFPAC/COMNAVSURFLANTINST 3502.7, Surface Force Training and Readiness Manual, 1 Nov 2018 — [All Hands OPTASK reference](https://allhands.navy.mil/Portals/1/Optask/references/COMNAVSURFPAC_COMNAVSURFLANTINST-3502_7-1Nov18-Surface_Force_Training_Readiness_Manual.pdf?ver=W4gxIOXq_F2u55iCVjiVzQ%3D%3D) (snippet; 403). DVIDS confirms the SFTRM "was published on November 1, 2018" — [DVIDS, 4 Mar 2019](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/312832). A Scribd upload is titled "SURFLANTINST 3502.7A - Surface Force Readiness and Training Manual (SFTRM)", which suggests a 3502.7A revision exists. Only the title was seen — [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/457053639/SURFLANTINST-3502-7A-Surface-Force-Readiness-and-Training-Manual-SFTRM-pdf). No 3502.7B was found. The predecessor was the Surface Force Readiness Manual, COMNAVSURFPAC/SURFLANTINST 3502.3 — [Brainscape](https://www.brainscape.com/flashcards/6447402/packs/10135998) (weak). **Correction:** "SFTRM, COMNAVSURFPAC/COMNAVSURFLANTINST 3502.7 (1 Nov 2018); a 3502.7A revision appears to exist; current revision unconfirmed."

**READ-E**
- **CONFIRMED, but DATED (2021).** Spec: "a TYCOM-led assessment called READ-E 4. USS Bulkeley's included six events: main reduction gear assessment, light-off assessment, crew certification, dock trials and fast cruise, navigation assessment, contractor sea trials". DVIDS (posted 6 Feb 2021; crew cert 11-12 Jan 2021) says: "READ-E 4 is a Type Commander-led assessment of the ship's readiness to exit the maintenance phase". It is "nominally conducted in the last three weeks of the maintenance phase". The events are listed as main reduction gear **health** assessment, light off assessment, crew certification, dock trials and fast cruise, **squadron/group staff** navigation assessment, and contractor sea trials. Crew certification was run by the ISIC (DESRON 2) reviewing watchbills, not by ATG. — [DVIDS, Bulkeley Completes Crew Certification](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/388540/bulkeley-completes-crew-certification). **Correction:** tag [S, DATED 2021]. Add "nominally the last three weeks of the maintenance phase" (this is a usable generator rule). Note that crew certification is ISIC/DESRON-run and the navigation assessment is squadron/group-staff-run.
- **Context, not in spec (spec is incomplete).** The 2018 SFTRM lists READ-E 1 through READ-E 7 as separate evaluations. Completing READ-E 1, 2 and 3 is among the Basic Phase certification requirements. Third-party assessors grade Certification Exercises, "at a minimum READ-E 3 and READ-E 5" — [SFTRM 2018](https://allhands.navy.mil/Portals/1/Optask/references/COMNAVSURFPAC_COMNAVSURFLANTINST-3502_7-1Nov18-Surface_Force_Training_Readiness_Manual.pdf?ver=W4gxIOXq_F2u55iCVjiVzQ%3D%3D) (snippet, fragmentary). **Recommendation:** the generator models only READ-E 4. It should note that other READ-Es exist. READ-E numbers do not track a simple chronological order.
- **Minor.** A flashcard set lists READ-E 4 as five events (LOA, Crew Cert, Dock Trials/Fast Cruise, Nav Assessment, KTR Sea Trials), without the MRG assessment — [Brainscape SFTRM flashcards](https://www.brainscape.com/flashcards/sftrm-11200249/packs/19523078) (weak source). Keep the six-event DVIDS list.
- **CONFIRMED with caveat.** Spec §4 generator order: "MRG assessment → light-off → crew cert → dock trials / fast cruise → nav assessment → sea trials". This matches the order DVIDS *lists* the events, but the article does not say they are strictly sequential. Light-off assessments are ISIC-executed checks of material condition, knowledge and firefighting before light-off — [SWOS FTS slide](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/cmd/fts/Fts20/tsld023.htm) (DATED). **Correction:** tag the strict ordering [PROP], since it is inferred from a listing.

**Basic Phase anecdotes**
- **CONFIRMED, but DATED (2018) and FDNF-specific.** Spec: "USS Curtis Wilbur certified 18 mission areas in 18 weeks". Curtis Wilbur finished Basic Phase on 30 July 2018. The crew "certified in 18 mission areas over 18 weeks", evaluated by Afloat Training Group Western Pacific. The training officer said CONUS-based ships get 25 weeks for the same work — [INDOPACOM/navy.mil, Curtis Wilbur Completes Basic Phase](https://www.pacom.mil/Media/News/News-Articles/Article/1589535/curtis-wilbur-completes-basic-phase/) (snippet; 403). **Correction:** "Curtis Wilbur (FDNF Japan) certified 18 mission areas in 18 weeks in 2018; CONUS ships are allotted ~25 weeks [S-SNIP, DATED]." This matters if the generator sizes Basic Phase duration.
- **CONFIRMED, but DATED (June 2020) and not a pure mission-area count.** Spec: "USS John S. McCain was evaluated across 23 areas". McCain completed Basic Phase on 2 June 2020, announced 16 June 2020. The crew was evaluated "in 23 different areas including seamanship, navigation, supply, engineering, electronic warfare, medical, and damage control" — [Janes](https://www.janes.com/defence-news/news-detail/mccain-crew-completes-basic-certification); [USNI News, 16 Jun 2020](https://news.usni.org/2020/06/16/uss-john-mccain-back-to-operations-almost-3-years-after-fatal-collision); [Stripes](https://stripes.com/theaters/asia_pacific/three-years-after-deadly-collision-uss-john-s-mccain-is-ready-to-carry-on-1.633922) (snippets). **Correction:** tag [DATED]. Note that the 23 "areas" mix warfare areas with supply, medical and other non-warfare programs, and that this was a post-collision return-to-operations case.
- **OUTDATED framing (2018/2019 change, not 2024-2026).** Spec: "Ships can now certify early once they demonstrate proficiency". The SFTRM revisions (published 1 Nov 2018) "permit ships' crews to certify early when they demonstrate full proficiency during Certification Events (CEs)", which "returns remaining training time to the commanding officer" — [DVIDS, CNSP, 4 Mar 2019](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/312832) (fetched). No newer 2024-2026 early-certification policy was found. **Correction:** "Since the Nov 2018 SFTRM, crews may certify early on demonstrating full proficiency during Certification Events [S, DATED 2019]." Drop "now". Also adopt the real term **Certification Event (CE)** for events like Mission C.

**ATG role**
- **CONFIRMED (supports the spec's "SFTRM / ATG" framing).** The 2018 SFTRM "puts a big 'T' (Training) and little 'a' (assessment) into Afloat Training Groups (ATG)". ATG first certifies the ship's watch teams, then the ship's training team — [DVIDS, 4 Mar 2019](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/312832). ATG Western Pacific evaluated Curtis Wilbur's Basic Phase — [INDOPACOM](https://www.pacom.mil/Media/News/News-Articles/Article/1589535/curtis-wilbur-completes-basic-phase/) (snippet). For READ-E 4, the ISIC/DESRON runs crew cert — [DVIDS Bulkeley](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/388540/bulkeley-completes-crew-certification).

**Integrated Phase events**
- **CONFIRMED (order).** Spec: "Integrated Phase: Group Sail with SWATT, then COMPTUEX". On 28 Jan 2026, CSG-10 (USS Gonzalez, DESRON 22) "conducted integrated underway operations in support of Group Sail" while preparing "for Composite Training Unit Exercise (COMPTUEX)". It credits the training environment provided by CSG-4 — [DVIDS, Jan 2026](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/557024) (fetched).
- **Partly WRONG (phase placement of SWATT).** Navy sources say "SWATT bridges the gap between the basic and integrated training phases". It is an advanced-phase cruiser-destroyer event covering SUW, ASW, IAMD and information warfare, run so that communications and networking problems are fixed before COMPTUEX — [Surface Warfare Advanced Tactical Training coverage](https://www.surfpac.navy.mil/Portals/54/Documents/Digital%20Story/2023/6-Jun/Why-the-Navy-Loves-to-SWATT–What-You-Should-Know-V2.pdf); [USNI News, 2016](https://news.usni.org/?p=24437) (snippets). Sources disagree on length: 4 weeks, 3 weeks, or "five days of in-port training before 14 days underway" — [Navy Times, Jul 2018](https://www.navytimes.com/training-sim/2018/07/10/us-navy-adding-new-training-to-pre-deployment-work-ups/) (snippet). **Correction:** model SWATT as an Advanced-phase event that precedes, or is sometimes embedded in, Group Sail. Keep Mission A's name "Group Sail / SWATT AW serial", but note that SWATT itself is the advanced bridge. Keep the order SWATT → Group Sail → COMPTUEX.
- **UNVERIFIABLE (quote).** Spec: COMPTUEX is "the definitive, final training". This is attributed to a navy.mil Gonzalez press release (Article 2263015, ~2020), which returned 403. The fetched 2026 DVIDS Gonzalez article does not use that phrase — [DVIDS 557024](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/557024). Keep the quote, but tag it [S-SNIP, DATED].

**Lapse and periodicity**
- **CONFIRMED (lapse), and a better source exists.** Spec: "certifications expire and need revalidation (implied by Aug 2026 Proceedings)". GAO testimony found that "as of June 2017, 37 percent of the warfare certifications for cruiser and destroyer crews based in Japan had expired", up from 7% in Jan 2015. Over two-thirds had been expired five months or more — [GAO-17-798T (7 Sep 2017)](https://www.gao.gov/assets/690/688594.pdf); [GAO-17-809T highlights (19 Sep 2017)](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-17-809t-highlights.pdf); [Defense Daily](https://defensedaily.com/gao-finds-37-percent-japan-based-destroyercruiser-warfare-certifications-expired/?fullview=1) (snippets). The Aug 2026 Proceedings article ("Navy Readiness Requires Flexible, Weighted Certifications") returned 403 and could not be checked — [USNI Proceedings](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/2026/august/navy-readiness-requires-flexible-weighted-certifications). **Correction:** cite GAO-17-798T/809T as the [S] basis for expiry (DATED 2017). The `EXPIRED` status enum value is then sourced.
- **Related.** DoD IG (DODIG-2020-056, Feb 2020) reviewed DDG readiness assessments from 2013-2018. It notes the updated SFRM has checkpoints "to ensure ships are Basic Phase certified before deployment" — [DoD IG](https://www.dodig.mil/reports.html/Article/2074324/audit-of-readiness-of-arleigh-burke-class-destroyers-dodig-2020-056/) (snippet). This supports Basic Phase certifications as prerequisites for later events (`prerequisite_certs`).

### Inferences
- The `cert_status.csv` example (`certified_date 2026-05-20`, `expires 2027-05-20`) implies 12-month validity. No public source for SFTRM certification periodicity was found, so 12 months is unsupported. It should be a named, [UNV]-tagged parameter in `delay_model_params.yaml`, not a hard-coded year.
- Mission C, "Basic Phase ASW certification event", should use the SFTRM term "Certification Event (CE)", sourced in [DVIDS 2019](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/312832).
- The X6 scenario (a CMAV extension invalidating a certification start) is consistent with sourced practice. Certification events depend on equipment and crew availability, and SFTRM early or late certification is flexible.

### Gaps
- The current SFTRM revision letter and its full DDG certification list, with periodicities (the 2018 PDF and the Scribd 3502.7A were not readable).
- Whether READ-E numbering or content changed after 2018.
- The exact SWATT-to-Group-Sail relationship in 2025-2026 CSG workups. Sources describe SWATT both as a standalone bridge and as conducted during Group Sail.
- The text of the Aug 2026 Proceedings article (403).

## Q3. Maintenance availabilities (spec 15): CMAV, SRA/DSRA/SIA/PIA definitions, BAWP, NMD/NMD-R/NDE, milestones, GAO and Navy on-time statistics, RMC roles

### Takeaway
Spec 15 has four material errors:
- The "two-week CMAV every four months" figure is an LCS-labelled line from an old JFMM training note (Rev A Ch-4). A DDG CMAV is about 3-4 weeks.
- SIA is not the forward-deployed equivalent of a CMAV. It is a CNO-scheduled, non-docking FDNF availability of about 60-75 days.
- GAO-20-86's "50 of 71" covers only *overseas-homeported* ships. The 75% / 33,700-day figure is GAO-20-257T, not GAO-20-86.
- "Only A-120 and package lock are publicly attested" is wrong. A-720, A-360, A-240, A-120, A-75 and A-35 lock and definitization milestones are all publicly documented.

The FY2025 on-time outcome is now known: RMCs hit **41%** against a 71% goal.

### Cited Findings
**CMAV and SIA**
- **WRONG for DDGs / OUTDATED source.** Spec: "CMAV ... 'notionally a two-week availability, every four months' [S] (NAVSEA JFMM training notes)". The training-notes document is headed "COMFLTFORCOMINST 4790.3 REV A CH-4". ## Q4. INSURV (spec 18): periodicity, inspection types, annual report, finding vocabulary

### Takeaway
Spec 18 is outdated or wrong on three points:
- Periodicity: in-service ships have been on a **3-year** INSURV Material Inspection cycle since 1 Oct 2019 (Title 10 says "once every three years, if practicable"), not 48-60 months.
- Latest report: the newest public annual report is **FY2024** (dated 1 Mar 2025), not FY2023.
- Vocabulary: severity is not MAJOR/MINOR. INSURV uses **starred deficiencies** and **EOC (0.0-1.0) / IFOM** scores.
- "FTA" is not an INSURV inspection type found in any source. The real types are MI, AT, FCT, GMI, plus MSC's SMART.

### Cited Findings

The periodicity history:
