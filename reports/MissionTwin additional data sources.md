# MissionTwin needs twelve more feeds to forecast

To build its full version, MissionTwin needs about a dozen data sources beyond SKED, OMMS-NG and R-ADM. They fall into four groups. **Supply:** R-Supply requisitions and COSAL allowances, MILSTRIP supply-status codes, NAVSUP One Touch Support (OTS) and Global Distance Support Center (GDSC) CASREP requisition status, and NAVSUP WSS wholesale stock. **Readiness:** CASREP messages, DRRS readiness ratings, the MFOM material-readiness tree and ROC/POE mission assignments. **Operations:** the OFRP employment schedule, SFTRM certification events, CNO availability milestones in NMD/NDE, and the free-text daily reports a CO actually reads. **Fielding:** no source system publishes a field-level schema, so a fielded tool would consume them as Jupiter/Advana data products rather than plug into each one. The Navy funds L-IDE, its Palantir-based logistics data environment, explicitly to link logistics data into Advana and Jupiter. In January 2026 the Department of War gave CDAO authority to direct release of data to cleared developers. As of November 2026 the fleet runs a mix of old and new systems. R-Supply is still in sustainment, and NOSS's first commercial release is targeted for FY2027. N-MRO was restarted after its Lockheed Martin agreement was cancelled in April 2025. GenAI.mil is the mandated CUI/IL5 generative-AI platform, and it prohibits PII. Two findings shape the design more than any single feed. First, the most predictive parts signal is the two-letter supply-status code (BA, BB, BV and so on), not anything inside R-Supply. Second, a fleet-wide forecast of which ships cannot do which missions closely resembles SECRET readiness reporting, so classification by compilation is a real risk. For the hackathon, the right move is about 20 synthetic files built on real code vocabularies, each visibly labeled with its own provenance, and engine assumptions calibrated against a small set of GAO and Navy figures.

A note on evidence before the details. All sources are unclassified and public. Many primary documents blocked automated retrieval (NETC 3040.1A, the DLM 4000.25 AP7.16 status-code appendix, the Advana transformation memo, gao.gov and several navy.mil pages, and the Sabre Systems DRRS training library). Facts drawn only from search-result excerpts are marked **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. Material older than about 2022, including much of the CASREP format detail from 1990s training manuals, is marked **[DATED]**. Claims that rest on general knowledge rather than a fetched source are marked **[UNVERIFIED]**. Every file layout, code legend and distribution designed for the hackathon is marked **[PROPOSED]**. Proposed formats are never official Navy formats.

## Parts forecasting runs on MILSTRIP status codes, not R-Supply internals

**R-Supply is still the operative afloat supply system in late 2026.** NAVSEA describes the OMMS-NG/R-Supply database as "the preferred and, most commonly used" channel for delivering COSAL data, because it automates updates to a ship's installed-equipment records. NAVSEA also defines Storeroom Items as "allowed spares or repair parts carried in the ship's storeroom(s)" ([NAVSEA PAFOS Ch. 6, Feb 2025](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/NSLC/PAFOS%20Chapter%206%20finalize%2028%20Feb%2025.docx)). A 2025 Navy solicitation lists R-Supply "Patriot" in Unit Level (small ships) and Force Level (big decks) variants alongside NALCOMIS and OMMS-NG, which shows the system was in active sustainment in 2025 ([orangeslices.ai, N00244-25-Q-0017](https://orangeslices.ai/?p=112983)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. A contractor posting says R-Supply interfaces with NALCOMIS, OMMS-NG, MFOM and NMRO ([ClearanceJobs](https://clearancejobs.com/jobs/8165757/ntcss-relational-supply-subject-matter-expert-sme)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. Its successor is **NOSS**, the supply component of NOBLE. One Network Enterprises won about $42.6M in November 2020 to prototype it ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/it-networks/2020/11/06/us-navy-awards-43m-contract-for-autonomous-supply-chain-management/)). The FY2027 budget rendering says NOSS will replace *ashore* R-Supply, with Minimum Viable Commercial Release 1 targeted for FY2027 ([HigherGov P-1 line 2611](https://www.highergov.com/budget/naval-tactical-command-support-system-ntcss-cecc987/)) **[secondary rendering of budget justification]**. No public 2025-2026 source gives an afloat NOSS fielding date or an R-Supply sunset. Synthetic supply files should therefore carry a `system_of_record` field (RSUPPLY or NOSS), so a transition can be shown without asserting dates. No official R-Supply table or field names are public.

The richest public structure is the DoD-wide MILSTRIP/DLMS standard that R-Supply implements. Status-code definitions now live in DLM 4000.25 Volume 2, Appendix AP7.16 (Change 18, September 2024) ([DLA](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/Manuals/DLM/V2/0207.16-v2a7.16.docx)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. A secondary mirror lists **33 B-series, 33 C-series and 28 D-series codes**. They include **BA** (processing for shipment from stock, with an estimated shipping date), **BB** (backordered against a due-in), **BC** (backordered, long delay), **BV** (on contract for direct vendor delivery), **BZ** (direct-delivery procurement in process), **B3** (RDD unrealistic), and rejections **CB/CG/CJ/CU** ([logtool.com](https://logtool.com/Toolbox/dod-supply-status-codes)). In DLMS, BA status carries the estimated shipping date in CCYYMMDD form ([ADC 1049](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC_1049_ESAFAT_Supply_Status.pdf)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**.

Urgency comes from the two-digit **priority designator (01 highest to 15 lowest)**. PD 01-03 forms Issue Priority Group I ([tpub](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/12654/css/Requisition-Handling-Precedence-131.htm)) **[DATED]**. CASREP-driven orders are NORS requisitions, and **RDD "999" marks C2/C3/C4 CASREP requisitions** ([tpub](https://navyadministration.tpub.com/14241/css/Preparation-Of-Nors-Nmcs-Requisitions-113.htm)) **[DATED]**. Time Definite Delivery categories set the on-time reference line. Category 1 is PD 01-03. Category 2 is triggered by an RDD under 8 days out (CONUS) or under 21 days out (OCONUS). The default CONUS in-transit segment is 5 days ([ADC 1114](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/DLMS/ADC/ADC_1114_Change_MRA_Times_based_on_TDD.pdf)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**.

Several common format details are **[UNVERIFIED]**. These include the 14-character document number (DoDAAC + Julian date + serial), afloat DoDAACs formed as "R" plus the UIC, the full F/AD-by-urgency priority matrix, the DIC AE_/AS_ status transactions, and the COG and RIC mappings. The engine still has a clear sourced backbone: the status code tells it which delay regime a part is in. BA with an ESD is near term. BB depends on a due-in. BC, BV and BZ are procurement tails. C-series codes mean the order failed and must be re-requisitioned.

Visibility ashore runs through NAVSUP. The GDSC page offers "Stock Availability, Requisition Status" and a dedicated **"One Touch Support High Priority (CASREP) Requisition Status"** link, and lists a Priority Material Office ([NAVSUP GDSC](https://www.navsup.navy.mil/Mission-Areas/Global-Distance-Support-Center/)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. OTS access requires a DoD PKI certificate registered in the NAVSUP Master Directory ([NETC NROTC supply binder, Aug 2025](https://www.netc.navy.mil/Portals/46/NSTC/NROTC/01.%20Supply%20Binder/03.%20Section%203/3-15%20Material%20Obligation%20Validation%20Aug%202025.pdf)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. No public OTS export layout exists.

NAVSUP WSS supports **almost 300 deployable ships** ([DVIDS](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/548939)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]** and treats CASREPs as "high-visibility, high-priority requisitions." In April 2024, USS Gridley filed **seven CASREPs traced to a single ethernet switch** for the AN/SQQ-89. The ISEA confirmed six were needed; WSS filled five from the shelf and got one from program office IWS-5 ([DVIDS](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/469139)). That case is a template for MissionTwin. The fix ranking should first search wholesale stock, then program-office assets, and only then sister ships. It should also deduplicate CASREPs that share a root part.

The "borrow from a nearby ship" option is where public data is thinnest. **GAO-23-106440 found per-ship maintenance cannibalizations rising in nine of ten surface ship classes from FY2015 to FY2021**, attributed partly to spare-parts shortages ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106440); [GAO testimony](https://www.gao.gov/assets/820/817121.txt)). In 2011, about one ship in two reported cannibalizing to become ready or pass inspections, even though the Navy called the practice a "last resort" ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/dodbuzz/2011/07/19/report-parts-swapping-is-endemic-across-navy)) **[DATED]**. Earlier GAO work found Navy cannibalization data understated by as much as 50% (aviation) ([GAO-02-86](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-GAO-02-86/html/GAOREPORTS-GAO-02-86.htm)) **[DATED]**.

There is no public tool that shows other ships' onboard stock. The current ship-level approval chain is also unconfirmed: OPNAV 4440.19E is listed as inactive ([GlobalSpec](https://standards.globalspec.com/std/424385/opnav-4440-19e)), and P-485 and the JFMM were not retrieved. A blog distinguishes **cross-decking from storeroom stock** from **cannibalizing installed equipment** ([Navy Matters](https://navy-matters.blogspot.com/2016/04/cannibalization.html)) **[UNVERIFIED]**. MissionTwin should keep that split because the two options cost very differently. Cross-decking only reduces the donor's spares. Cannibalizing degrades the donor, creates the double work GAO describes, and may trigger a donor CASREP. Any cannibalization rate the engine uses should be presented as a lower bound.

## CASREPs and readiness ratings define what "mission ready" means

The CASREP is MissionTwin's primary event stream. A casualty is a deficiency that **cannot be corrected within 48 hours** and reduces mission capability ([NETCINST 3040.1A, 2021](https://www.netc.navy.mil/Portals/46/NETC/inst/30401A.pdf)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. Reports come as INITIAL, UPDATE, CORRECT (CASCOR) and CANCEL (CASCAN). Follow-ups must reference the INITIAL's DTG. **Category 2, 3 and 4** run from minor degradation to loss of a primary mission, and category 1 is not used ([tpub gunners 277](https://www.tpub.com/gunners/277.htm); [276](https://www.tpub.com/gunners/276.htm)) **[DATED]**.

The older training manuals describe the lifecycle, and that lifecycle supplies the labels a forecasting model needs ([tpub gunners 275](https://www.tpub.com/gunners/275.htm); [276](https://www.tpub.com/gunners/276.htm)) **[DATED]**:

- **Set structure:** a CASREP is a serialized set-based message (MSGID first, serials 1-999 per unit). The ESTIMATE set carries the estimated time of repair, and ASSIST flags outside help.
- **INITIAL:** one casualty per report and only one open CASREP per equipment item. RMKS gives a 30-day availability window for receiving parts or assistance.
- **UPDATE:** sent when the repair date, parts status or assistance needs change. It always repeats the CASUALTY and ESTIMATE sets.
- **Closure:** the CASCOR records parts receipt dates, **supply-caused delay in hours** and man-hours. A CASCAN records deferral to a scheduled availability.

The current NTTP 1-03.1 field order and parts-set layout were not retrieved and remain **[UNVERIFIED]**. GAO-12-887 adds three facts that matter for design ([GAO-12-887](https://www.gao.gov/assets/650/648722.txt)). **CASREP data is owned by Fleet Forces and Pacific Fleet**, and GAO pulled it from MFOM. Individual-ship CASREPs are classified when they reveal warfighting capability, while roll-ups above ship level are not. Reporting is inconsistent across ships because of leadership philosophy and crew experience.

**The readiness system's name is itself contested in the sources.** One body of material describes DRRS-N as the Navy's capabilities-based system of record. Under that description, units rate Navy Mission Essential Tasks (NMETs) Yes, Qualified Yes or No and show PESTO resource pillars, per NTRP 1-03.5 (Feb 2016) ([NTRP 1-03.5](https://elearning.sabresystems.com/drrsS/instructions/NTRP%201-03.5%20DRRS-N%20REPORTING%20MANUAL%20EDITION%20FEBRUARY%202016.pdf)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. GAO adds that reporting is monthly or within 24 hours of a significant change, and that the data are classified ([GAO-12-887](https://www.gao.gov/assets/650/648722.txt)). But USFF Instruction 3501.6 (June 2020) states that **since 1 October 2019, the OSD-hosted DRRS-S has been the Navy's primary readiness input tool**, that the ashore-hosted DRRS-N was retired, and that DRRS handles data up to SECRET ([USFFINST 3501.6](https://elearning.sabresystems.com/drrsS/instructions/USFFINST%203501_6%2014June2020.pdf)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY, DATED]**. The best reading is that "DRRS-N" survives as the Navy reporting construct and vocabulary (NMETs, PESTO), while the input and display tool is DRRS-S. DoD DRRS itself is being modernized, not replaced: DoDD 7730.65 (May 2023) assigns its modernization to USD(P&R) ([DoDD 7730.65](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodd/773065p.pdf)), and DRRS data still feed the semi-annual readiness report to Congress per a September 2026 CRS update ([CRS IF12240](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12240.html)).

The key mechanical rule comes from a carrier brief, so surface rules may differ. **A Category 4 CASREP forces the associated MET to N and the SORTS equipment level to C-3 or lower. A Category 3 forces Q or lower and C-2 or lower** ([DRRS-S CVN SORTS brief, 2023](https://elearning.sabresystems.com/drrsS/presentations/DRRS-S%20CVN%20SORTS_01Jan2023.pdf)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. This is the hinge of MissionTwin's value. DRRS shows today's Y/Q/N. MissionTwin projects Y/Q/N per mission at day 14 and day 28, from ETRs, parts status, availability slips and qualification expirations.

**MFOM is the closest public analog to the mission-to-equipment map MissionTwin needs.** Its patent describes a hierarchy that runs from warfare mission and Navy Tasks down through functional area, system, component and part. Equipment is scored 0.0-1.0. Scores roll up by weighted average, or by a modified average when a "critical" child exists. **"The Ship's 3M System and CASREPS are the only reporting systems that directly feed"** equipment status into MFOM. 2-Kilo equipment status maps to scores (status 0 = 1.0, 1 = 0.8, 3 = 0.6, 2 = 0). A "Time Accelerator" weights repairs by weeks to the next deployment ([MFOM patent US20120215734A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20120215734A1/en)). MFOM sends 0-100 warfare-area indices to DRRS, banded **80-100 green, 60-79 yellow, 0-59 red** ([MFOM patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20120215734A1/en)). AWN, the OMMS-NG replacement, is MFOM's shipboard segment ([GlobalSecurity/NNS 2010](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2010/04/mil-100416-nns03.htm)) **[DATED]**. VSolvit holds the MFOM recompete supporting NIWC Atlantic ([VSolvit](https://vsolvit.com/vsolvit-awarded-mfom-recompete-contract/)). MFOM reached initial operating capability in October 2007 ([USFFC brief](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20090109150418/http:/www.acq.osd.mil/log/mpp/uid/USFFC_ship_materiel_condition_metrice_model.pdf)) **[DATED]**. The patent is from about 2012, so the current scoring may differ.

**ROC/POE documents supply the authoritative "which missions does this class owe" layer.** Each class-specific OPNAVINST 3501.xxx assigns primary and secondary mission areas (AW, ASW, SUW and so on) and the required capability by readiness condition. ROC and POE together also drive manpower requirements ([archived DON 3501 series](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130214013851/http://doni.daps.dla.mil/Directives/03000%20Naval%20Operations%20and%20Readiness/03-500%20Training%20and%20Readiness%20Services/3501.160C.pdf)) **[DATED, 2013 snapshot]**. OPNAVINST 3501.115F (2023) updated required readiness levels ([GlobalSpec](https://standards.globalspec.com/std/13083290/opnav-3501-115f)). The real table columns and the Full/Minimal/Limited capability legend are **[UNVERIFIED]**.

**INSURV** contributes a slower signal: material inspections roughly every 48-60 months ([GAO-12-887](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-12-887)), plus a releasable annual report ([INSURV FY2023](https://www.insurv.usff.navy.mil/Portals/41/2023%20Annual%20Report_Releasable_FINAL.pdf)). The earlier research established the training pillar flow (TORIS to NTIMS to DRRS, with TFOM as the core metric) ([COMNAVSURFORINST 3502.1D](https://navytribe.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/comnavsurforinst-3502-1d-1.pdf)). No public definition of SFOM, OFOM or a "Navy Readiness Kill Chain" was found.

On "calibration data," the research covered statistical calibration figures, collected in the calibration table below. It did not cover TMDE/METCAL calibration records. If the team means equipment calibration status (an out-of-calibration test set blocking a repair or a certification), the notes give no sourced system name or format. Any `tmde_calibration.csv` would be wholly **[PROPOSED]** and should be presented that way.

## Schedules set the deadline; availabilities and certifications move it

Every readiness forecast needs a "ready by when, for what" target, and that comes from the OFRP schedule. OPNAVINST 3000.15A and the Fleet companion COMUSFLTFORCOM/COMPACFLTINST 3000.15B (October 2020) define the phases: maintenance, basic, integrated/advanced and sustainment ([BUMEDINST 3000.1](https://www.med.navy.mil/Portals/62/Documents/BUMED/Directives/Instructions/3000.1.pdf)). The **36-month notional cycle "often ends up clocking in at 42 to 44 months,"** mostly because modernization runs late ([USNI News, Feb 2026](https://news.usni.org/2026/02/12/improving-maintenance-timelines-key-to-on-time-strike-group-deployments-cno-says)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. Schedules move through a Fleet Forces quarterly schedule review, which a 2020s TYCOM scheduler job posting confirms ([LCE posting](https://job-openings.youthopps.monster.com/tycom-cno-availability-maintenance-scheduler-coordinator-norfolk-va-us-life-cycle-engineering/5697fff1-2384-43b4-bf3d-cac34fbb2fe8)). Older SWOS material adds several ship-level details ([SWOS 72-2](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-2.html); [SWOS 72-4](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-4.html)) **[DATED]**:

- The Operations Officer owns the ship's schedule.
- The long-range schedule rolls 18-24 months ahead and is updated quarterly.
- The quarterly scheduling conference locks underway inputs about 6 months ahead.
- Employment codes include RFS, UPK and HOLUPK.

**No public source names the current fleet scheduling IT system or its export format**, and "Navy Scheduling System" could not be confirmed **[UNVERIFIED]**. For MissionTwin's 2-4 week horizon, the schedule is mostly fixed. The forecast risk is that availabilities and certifications slip inside a fixed window.

Certifications carry the equipment and qualification prerequisites. Leaving maintenance requires a TYCOM-led assessment the Navy calls **READ-E 4**. USS Bulkeley's included six events: main reduction gear assessment, light-off assessment, crew certification, dock trials and fast cruise, a navigation assessment, and contractor sea trials ([navy.mil](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/Article/2495853/bulkeley-completes-crew-certification/)). Basic Phase mission-area certifications follow. **USS Curtis Wilbur certified 18 mission areas in 18 weeks** ([navy.mil](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/News-Stories/display-news/Article/2249867/curtis-wilbur-completes-basic-phase)), and USS John S. McCain was evaluated across 23 areas ([CPF](https://www.cpf.navy.mil/Newsroom/News/Article/2637622/uss-john-s-mccain-certified-for-return-to-operations)). Ships can certify early once they demonstrate proficiency ([CPF](https://www.cpf.navy.mil/Newsroom/News/Article/2664387/rewarding-a-culture-of-excellence-ships-crews-can-now-earn-certification-early)). Group Sail with SWATT and then COMPTUEX follow in the Integrated Phase. COMPTUEX is "the definitive, final training" to judge deployment readiness ([navy.mil](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/Press-Releases/display-pressreleases/Article/2263015/uss-gonzalez-begins-comptuex/); [DVIDS](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/557024)). An August 2026 Proceedings article argues for "flexible, weighted certifications," which implies today's certifications lapse and need revalidation ([USNI Proceedings](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/2026/august/navy-readiness-requires-flexible-weighted-certifications)). The current SFTRM text, the full DDG certification list and any per-certification equipment or watch-team prerequisite matrix were not found. The prerequisites MissionTwin encodes (for example, the AMW cert requires SPY, Aegis, CIWS and qualified CIC teams) are **[PROPOSED]** inferences from event names.

Maintenance availabilities are where schedules break. Between CNO availabilities, **CMAVs are "notionally a two-week availability, every four months"** ([NAVSEA JFMM training notes](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/JFMM_Training_Notes_D4.docx)). DSRAs on SAM.gov suggest durations of about 15-18 months ([SAM.gov](https://sam.gov/opp/83c7cce5b34a40a19df81639bd8c13d8/view)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY, UNVERIFIED durations]**. SURFMEPP's Baseline Availability Work Package defines each ship's work across the cycle ([NAVSEA SURFMEPP](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Home/Team-Ships/NAVSEA-21/SURFMEPP/Core-Products/)). NMD-R holds "planned milestone dates and avail costs" ([NAVSEA SNA 2020](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/Exhibits/SNA2020/SNA2020-SurfaceShipReadinessAndSustainment-CaptSteveMurray.pdf)). The original completion date is recorded in NDE at availability start ([JFMM training notes](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUBMEPP/JFMM/JFMM_Training_Notes.docx)). Only A-120 and "package lock" are publicly attested milestones ([LCE](https://www.lce.com/case-studies/lce-helps-u-s-navy-modernize-surface-ships/)). Overruns have been chronic. **50 of 71 surface ship maintenance periods (about 70%) ran long in 2014-2018** ([GAO-20-86](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-20-86-highlights.pdf)). The Navy reported delay days down 62% since 2019 to 2,633, with a goal of at least 71% of CNO availabilities on time by September 2025 ([Inside Defense](https://insidedefense.com/insider/navy-takes-lessons-cruise-lines-coast-guard-and-more-reduce-maintenance-delays)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY, Navy-reported; whether the goal was met is unknown]**. GAO-25-106990 (January 2025) cites spare parts, too few qualified maintainers and deferred maintenance as persistent problems across 149 combat surface ships ([GAO-25-106990](https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-25-106990)). The core MissionTwin scenario follows directly. A CMAV that slips in week 0 pushes light-off and crew certification into week 2 and a Basic Phase certification into week 4.

The CO's daily picture comes mostly from unstructured reports, which is why a PDF-ingest path matters. **Eight O'Clock Reports are "daily equipment status reports" to the CO through the XO, and are "usually locally generated forms"** whose format varies by command ([tpub ET](https://electronicstechnician.tpub.com/14085/css/Eight-Oclock-Reports-22.htm)). The XO's Plan of the Day sets the next day's sequence of events ([tpub](https://navyadvancement.tpub.com/14144/css/Officer-Of-The-Deck-In-Port-Cont-166.htm)). Off-ship messages are more structured. The OPSUM follows an OPGEN base format that commanders modify ([SWOS 72-9](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-9.html)) **[DATED]**. MOVREP uses numbered lines in Zulu time with update triggers at a 4-hour ETA change or 100 NM off track ([SWOS 72-7](https://man.fas.org/dod-101/navy/docs/swos/ops/72-7.html)) **[DATED]**. LOGREQ requests port and at-sea services through NAVSUP Fleet Logistics Centers ([NAVSUP FLC Pearl Harbor](https://www.navsup.navy.mil/NAVSUP-Enterprise/NAVSUP-FLC-Pearl-Harbor/Logistics-Service-Center/)). MOVREP and LOGREQ matter to the fix ranker because they say where a donor ship will be and when a part can physically arrive.

## A fielded MissionTwin would consume Jupiter data products under inherited ATOs

**Data access.** The credible enterprise path is through Advana and its Navy subtenant **Jupiter**, which launched in April 2020 with a Warfighting area covering readiness, maintenance and operations data ([WARU "What is Jupiter?"](https://www.waru.edu/sites/default/files/webform/documents/26876/What%20is%20Jupiter.pdf)). Task Force Hopper chose Advana-Jupiter as the surface force's common development environment and single data catalog. Jupiter lists datasets with their points of contact rather than hosting all of them, and the Surface Analytics Group is the readiness node ([FedScoop, 2022](https://fedscoop.com/ai-task-force-for-navy-surface-fleet-devising-comprehensive-data-catalog/)) **[DATED]**.

The supply and maintenance pipes are being built toward Jupiter. Palantir provides NOBLE's integrated data environment, and NTCSS, the largest legacy system it replaces, draws on more than 700 applications and databases ([FedScoop, 2020](https://fedscoop.com/palantir-navy-contract/)) **[DATED]**. The FY2027 L-IDE line **links logistics data with Advana and Jupiter**. It also records that NAMS and NOME were folded into N-MRO, which restarts with a COTS pilot after an April 2025 SECNAV memo cancelled the Lockheed Martin agreement ([HigherGov P-1 line 2611](https://www.highergov.com/budget/naval-tactical-command-support-system-ntcss-cecc987/)) **[secondary]**. That supersedes the earlier picture of N-MRO as a live Lockheed Martin/IFS program. In January 2026 a Deputy Secretary memo split Advana into a War Data Platform, a financial-management track and a WDP Application Services team that will standardize data access for agentic AI ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/01/13/hegseth-ai-tech-hubs-reorganization-dod-dow/)) **[memo text not retrieved]**. The concurrent AI strategy requires federated data catalogs and lets **CDAO direct release of Department data to cleared users with a valid purpose**, with denials justified to USD(R&E) ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/department-of-wars-ai-first-agenda-a-new-era-for-defense-contractors)) **[secondary]**.

The inferred access sequence has four steps:

1. Find each dataset in the DON federated catalog.
2. Request access from the named steward.
3. Execute a data-sharing agreement.
4. Build on Jupiter/WDP compute.

CDAO release authority is the escalation route if a steward refuses. No public onboarding form, WDP API specification, or list of which CASREP or NTCSS tables currently sit in Jupiter was found.

**Afloat hosting.** **CANES/ACS is the mandated development baseline for afloat applications** ([navy.mil, NAVADMIN 315-18 context](https://www.navy.mil/DesktopModules/ArticleCS/Print.aspx?PortalId=1&ModuleId=523&Article=2240143)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY, DATED]**. NTCSS itself is moving onto CANES hosting, with ship-set upgrades planned at 37 ships in FY2026 and 35 in FY2027 ([HigherGov](https://www.highergov.com/budget/naval-tactical-command-support-system-ntcss-cecc987/)). Containerized delivery is in evaluation, not fielded. In mid-2026 the Navy advanced Defense Unicorns' Unified Defense Stack to operational evaluation aboard ships and at shore sites, including air-gapped networks, under a NIWC Atlantic program with PMW 160 ([The Defense Post](https://thedefensepost.com/2026/07/20/defense-unicorns-us-navy-software/)). UDS Fleet launched on 2 June 2026 ([PR Newswire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260602cl73089/defense-unicorns-launches-uds-fleet-to-put-mission-operators-in-command-at-the-tactical-edge)). No ship names or ATO status were published. Defense Unicorns co-facilitates FleetHacker with CDAO ([DON CIO](https://www.doncio.navy.mil/ContentView.aspx?ID=20489)) **[secondary]**. A design that runs the forecast ashore in Jupiter, with an optional DDIL-tolerant Zarf/UDS bundle on CANES, fits the evaluation path the facilitators themselves run.

**Authorization and LLM use.** The fastest ATO route is inheritance. **RAISE** applies to all DON new starts and upgrades that use containerized methods, and it certifies the DevSecOps platform so the application only clears a gate ([DON CIO](https://www.doncio.navy.mil/(k3pdil3u5j2c0ui1hw1s3e55)/ContentView.aspx?id=16441)) **[SNIPPET-ONLY]**. The Overmatch Software Armory at NIWC Pacific uses RAISE to "dramatically reduce" time to ATO ([NIWC Pacific](https://www.niwcpacific.navy.mil/Technology/Overmatch-Software-Armory/)). Black Pearl claims a Navy ATO and RAISE platform status, though its wording is inconsistent ([blackpearl.navy.mil](https://blackpearl.navy.mil/)). For LLM features, a January 2026 Navy memo made **GenAI.mil the DON's mandated CUI/IL5 generative-AI platform, with transition by 30 April 2026; it allows CUI but prohibits PII and PHI** ([Military Times, Aug 2026](https://www.militarytimes.com/industry/techwatch/2026/08/31/the-militarys-chatgpt-is-now-live-via-the-pentagons-genai-platform/)). Models on GenAI.mil include Gemini for Government, ChatGPT Mil (from 31 August 2026) and Grok for Government. The platform is unclassified only. R-ADM crew data carries names and identifiers, so MissionTwin should keep its forecast core deterministic and statistical, and send only de-identified context to the LLM for explanation.

**Classification by compilation.** This is the hardest constraint. Single-ship CASREP, maintenance and supply records are generally CUI, but GAO notes that individual-ship CASREPs become classified when they reveal warfighting capability ([GAO-12-887](https://www.gao.gov/assets/650/648722.txt)). DRRS processes data up to SECRET ([USFFINST 3501.6](https://elearning.sabresystems.com/drrsS/instructions/USFFINST%203501_6%2014June2020.pdf)) **[DATED]**. A fleet-wide, forward-looking map of which ships will lose which missions looks like SECRET readiness output. The defensible plan is IL5 for a unit or squadron view and a SIPR deployment for the aggregated fleet picture, with the security classification guide owner engaged early. No public SCG text on CASREP aggregation was found, and OPNAVINST 5513.3C was cited but not retrieved ([NPS thesis](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1052795.pdf)).

The data should be seeded with deliberate inconsistencies, because reconciling fragmented sources is the real fleet problem:

- A CASREP DTG with no matching open JCN.
- Several CASREPs that trace to one NIIN, as in the Gridley case.
- A BA status whose ESD has passed.
- A daily report saying a tech assist "may slip" that no structured file reflects.
- A donor ship whose cross-deck would drop it below its allowance.
- A CMAV extension that invalidates a certification start date.

The proposed distributions from the notes are placeholders, not statistics, and the team should label them that way:

- **Status mix for open high-priority requisitions:** BA 30%, BB 30%, BV/BZ 15%, others.
- **Arrival medians:** about 4-7 days for BA in CONUS, 10-20 days deployed, 30-60 days for BB, and 90-180 days for procurement codes, with a tail beyond a year.
- **Cross-deck time:** 1-3 days when the donor is in company.
- **Availabilities:** most finish late.

## Conclusion

The research shows that MissionTwin's hardest problem is joining sources and getting authorized to use the joined data, not getting data at all. Each source holds one hinge of the forecast. The requisition status code tells the engine which delay regime a part is in. The CASREP ESTIMATE and CASCOR sets provide the repair-time labels. The MFOM-style critical-child tree turns a broken component into a lost mission. The certification chain turns a slipped CMAV into a missed COMPTUEX. None of these sources publishes a schema, and several are mid-migration. The tool that wins is therefore one whose join logic and assumption ledger can be audited, not one that claims to mirror official layouts.

The same join that creates the value also creates the compilation risk. A read-only forecaster that is unclassified at squadron scale could become SECRET at fleet scale. That argues for pitching MissionTwin to Surface Force sponsors as a tiered product: an IL5 squadron tool now, built on Jupiter data products under an inherited RAISE ATO, with a SIPR fleet view designed in from the start rather than bolted on later. Before the demo, the team should verify NTTP 1-03.1, the current SFTRM certification list, the DLM 4000.25 AP7.16 code table, and whether its synthetic donor-ship logic matches how TYCOMs actually approve cross-decks.
