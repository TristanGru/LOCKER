# Three NTCSS-era systems shape MissionTwin's synthetic data

SKED, OMMS-NG ("OMNS") and R-ADM ("RAD-M") are the three shipboard data sources MissionTwin should copy. Together they hold a ship's planned maintenance, its corrective maintenance backlog, and its crew and watchstation qualifications. **SKED** is NAVSEA's Planned Maintenance System (PMS) scheduler, built by Antech Systems; its latest public version is 3.2. **OMMS-NG** is the NTCSS application, in use since 2001, that records material deficiencies as OPNAV 4790/2K "2-Kilo" jobs, which roll up into the Current Ships Maintenance Project (CSMP). **R-ADM** is NTCSS Relational Administration, policy's authoritative afloat database for PQS, qualifications, certifications and watchbills. None of the three has a public file layout, export schema or data dictionary. The only safe templates are the published paper forms (OPNAV 4790/14 and /15 schedules, the LOEP, the 2-Kilo blocks) and the data categories named in policy. Everything below that looks like a CSV header is the research notes' own design, not an official layout. As of November 2026 all three are legacy, and their replacements are only partly fielded. AWN has replaced OMMS-NG on some ships but not all. NOBLE's N-MRO (Lockheed Martin and IFS) is the enterprise maintenance successor. Antech and NAVSEA are merging SKED, the PMS MIS and 3-M MDS into one application. No public source gives an NTCSS or R-ADM sunset date. For a hackathon, the realistic approach is source-agnostic synthetic files keyed on genuine identifiers: UIC, work center, JCN, MIP/MRC, NEC and PQS watchstation numbers. Each file should be labeled "synthetic, 3-M-like."

A note on evidence: many primary Navy PDFs (NAVSEAINST 4790.8D, the Surface Force Training and Readiness Manual, GAO-24-106525's full text) blocked automated retrieval. Several findings below therefore rest on search-engine excerpts of those documents. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** come from general Navy knowledge, not from a source found in this research.

## SKED schedules PMS from a fixed document chain

SKED is the scheduler for the Navy's Ships' 3-M Planned Maintenance System. Antech says it "developed NAVSEA's planned maintenance scheduling software, SKED," which views and prints maintenance procedures, automates PMS schedule creation and tracks accomplished maintenance for Sailors and work centers ([Antech](https://www.antechsystems.com/services/maintenance-management/sked/)). A NAVSEA page says SKED "provides the capability to schedule, display and manage planned maintenance events," and calls Ships' 3-M the "database of record for all maintenance-related execution data" (search excerpt) ([NAVSEA NUWC Keyport](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/home/warfare-centers/nuwc-keyport/nslc/products-services/maintenance-planning/)). The latest documented version is **SKED 3.2**, an upgrade from 3.1; no release date and no later version were found. Three user roles are sourced. **The 3-M Coordinator (3MC) creates PINs**, and Sailors use them to mark checks "complete" or "lost." The 3MC can also bulk-transfer users during a crew swap. Leadership sees a dashboard that colors **Accomplishment Ratings** by department, division and work center ([Antech SKED 3.2](https://www.antechsystems.com/?p=1171)). The governing manual is **NAVSEAINST 4790.8D** (17 June 2021), whose Appendix G reportedly covers SKED 3.2 scheduling ([NAVSEAINST 4790.8D](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/NAVINST/04790.008D.pdf)). OPNAV policy appears to have moved from 4790.4F to 4790.4G ([CNRFCINST 4790.1B](https://www.navyreserve.navy.mil/Portals/35/CNRFCINST%204790.1B%203M%20ASSESSMENT%20PROGRAM.pdf)). No source names SKED's program office; the claim that NSWC Corona maintains it is unconfirmed.

SKED's data follows a published document hierarchy, which is the strongest basis for realistic synthetic columns. The **LOEP (PMS 5 report)** lists a work center's MIPs and carries the report date, **force revision number, TYCOM, hull number and UIC, work center, MIP numbers and an equipment description**. The **MIP (OPNAV 4790/85)** gives periodicity codes, man-hours and minimum skill level for each equipment item. Each **MRC** says who (rate) does what, when, how and with what resources. The **EGL (4790/81)** gives the locations of identical equipment. The **Quarterly (4790/14)** and **Weekly (4790/15)** schedules break requirements into weeks, and the weekly schedule names the person assigned to each item ([tpub FC vol 1](https://www.tpub.com/fcv1/8.htm)). SKED 3.2 adds equipment-based schedules, "true interval scheduling," situational and metered triggers, Inactive Equipment Maintenance (IEM) periods, scheduled spot checks with scores, fleet-wide feedback reports and a forecast view ([Antech](https://www.antechsystems.com/?p=1171)). **The Navy issues a Force Revision four times a year**, and each one changes thousands of procedure documents ([Antech Tailored Force Revisions](https://www.antechsystems.com/services/maintenance-management/tailored-force-revisions/)). One detail matters for any deferral modeling: a search excerpt of the 3-M Manual says that **in SKED 3.2 PMS can no longer be deferred**, so unperformed checks count as lost or not accomplished ([NAVSEAINST 4790.8D](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/NAVINST/04790.008D.pdf)). This should be checked against Appendix G. In MissionTwin, "deferred maintenance" should come from the OMMS-NG/CSMP side, not from SKED.

Periodicity notation is only partly sourced. An aviation tag-guide title shows "M-1R: Monthly or every 600 hours, whichever [comes first]." That example shows a sequence number after the dash and an R suffix meaning calendar-plus-situational ([navyaviation tpub](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14310/css/Tag-Guide-List-Tgl-252.htm)). The full code table is **[UNVERIFIED]**: D, W, 2W, M, Q, S, A, 18M–36M, C, R and U. So are the MIP code shape (for example "4411/001-B6"), the 7-character MRC control number, the four-character work center codes (EE01, CE02, DC01) and the "accomplished ÷ scheduled" accomplishment-rate formula. On exports, the only sourced artifacts are printed schedules and MRCs, plus an **"upline report"** that sends "a virtual look of its SKED database to an external requesting authority" ([Antech](https://www.antechsystems.com/?p=1171)). No database engine, file extension or schema is public.

## OMMS-NG holds the deferred-maintenance backlog and the CASREP link

"OMNS" almost certainly means **OMMS-NG, the Organizational Maintenance Management System–Next Generation**; no Navy system named "OMNS" was found ([Acronym Finder](https://www.acronymfinder.com/Organizational-Maintenance-Management-System-_-Next-Generation-(US-Navy)-(OMMS_NG).html)). Other candidates are the pre-NG OMMS, its replacement AWN, NALCOMIS OMA (for aviation units) and NOBLE's NOME. GAO says the Navy began using OMMS-NG in **2001** "to document and report shipboard material deficiencies" (search excerpt) ([GAO-24-106525](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-24-106525.pdf)). NTCSS groups R-Supply, OMMS-NG and NALCOMIS, with R-ADM as the afloat personnel database ([DON CIO CHIPS, archived](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20170125022744/http:/www.doncio.navy.mil/(cyg51dffug4mbb45uqya2y45)/CHIPS/ArticleDetails.aspx?ID=4354)). Contractor postings describe entering, tracking and closing "maintenance and supply data in U.S. Navy OMMS-NG, R-Supply reports, and Microsoft Excel worksheets." That points to tight coupling with R-Supply and to Excel as the working export medium ([job posting](https://militaryspouse.dejobs.org/jacksonville-fl/process-control-analyst-us-navy-omms-ng/1DEC57136032487E84E27C90DFE25E91/job)). By standard 3-M convention, work center supervisors originate 2-Kilos, division officers and department heads review them, and the 3MC manages the CSMP. OMMS-NG-specific permissions are unconfirmed.

The atomic record is the **OPNAV 4790/2K**. Blocks 1–3 form the **Job Control Number (JCN)**: the ship's UIC (5 characters), the work center (4 alphanumeric characters) and a job sequence number (typically 4 digits) assigned by the work center supervisor. "From day one the job will always be identified by this combination" ([NAVSEA SERMC Capabilities Manual](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SERMC/Capabilities%20Manual%2022APR2022.pdf); [tpub ET Fig 13-15](https://electronicstechnician.tpub.com/14344/Figure-13-15-Ship-S-Maintenance-Action-Form-Opnav-4790-2K-479.html)). Block 41 is priority. Block 42 is **Type Availability: 1 Depot, 2 IMA, 3 Fleet Technical Support, 4 Ship's Force**. When a 2-Kilo documents a casualty, the remarks must **include the CASREP date-time group** ([SERMC Capabilities Manual 2023](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SERMC/Capabilities%20Manual%208FEB2023.pdf)). This is the only documented machine-joinable CASREP link found; no CASREP-generation module in OMMS-NG was confirmed. The equipment status code must match the configuration record, meaning the **APL/EIC/Location** ([JFMM excerpt](https://v.vibdoc.com/comusfltforcominst-479055419.html)). The 2K records deferrals and non-configuration completions, the 4790/CK records configuration changes, and the 4790/2P goes with IMA deferrals. The CO or an authorized representative signs original deferrals in Block E, the lead work center senior signs Block G, and the verifier signs Block H ([tpub 14310](https://navyaviation.tpub.com/14310/css/Maintenance-Planning-And-Estimating-Form-Opnav-4790-2P-268.htm)). Several things remain **[UNVERIFIED]**: the full code tables (status, when-discovered, deferral reason, cause, safety, equipment status, priority values), the exact EIC/APL formats, the CASREP categories (C2/C3/C4) and the CASREP report types (INITIAL/UPDATE/CORRECT/CANCEL). CASREPs themselves are governed by NTTP 1-03.1, which was not retrieved. The parts link should be modeled as requisition document numbers on the job, joined to R-Supply status. The field name is unconfirmed.

The integration story is mostly about broken or replaced interfaces. GAO reported that "just as the Navy stopped funding the interface between OMMS-NG and SKED, the Navy began to deploy an application to replace OMMS-NG (known as Automated Work Notification)" ([GAO-24-106525](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-24-106525.pdf)). **AWN is the shipboard segment of MFOM** ([GlobalSecurity/navy.mil 2010](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2010/04/mil-100416-nns03.htm)). OMMS-NG data feeds the 3-M Maintenance Data System (MDS) ashore ([Scribd 3M-301](https://www.scribd.com/doc/76030748/3M-301)). The names of the shore warehouses and their current ingest paths are unverified.

## R-ADM is policy's authoritative afloat source for quals and watchbills

"RAD-M" means **R-ADM**. Its Privacy Act name is "NTCSS Relational Administration (R-ADM)," and the notice says it "serves as the afloat personnel management system" on Navy ships and submarines ([Federal Register 2012-12045](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2012-05-18/html/2012-12045.htm)). Job postings often write it "RADM" alongside TORIS and FLTMPS ([ClearanceJobs](https://www.clearancejobs.com/jobs/8384304)). Outside these systems, RADM normally means Rear Admiral. The Surface Force Training and Readiness Manual (2018) designates R-ADM "the authoritative database for afloat activities with the NTCSS suite to capture individual unit-level training, including PQS, qualifications, and certifications." It requires every watchstander to be "qualified or interim qualified per R-ADM," and requires that "all watchbills will be created, managed, and maintained in R-ADM or another program that pulls data directly from the R-ADM database." It also allows that ships without R-ADM "may choose their method of tracking PQS" (search excerpts) ([SFTRM 3502.7](https://allhands.navy.mil/Portals/1/Optask/references/COMNAVSURFPAC_COMNAVSURFLANTINST-3502_7-1Nov18-Surface_Force_Training_Readiness_Manual.pdf?ver=W4gxIOXq_F2u55iCVjiVzQ%3D%3D)). Department leadership must check that personnel are entered correctly "by duty section, division, and work center" with the required PQS assignments ([Engineering Dept ORM](https://navalsafetycommand.navy.mil/portals/100/documents/Engineering-Dept-Organization-Regulations-Manual.pdf)).

The notice lists name, **SSN**, demographics, clearance, dependents, medical, military-record and education data. Records are retrieved by name and SSN, are sourced from the individual and the **Navy Training Management & Planning System**, and are destroyed when the member transfers ([Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2012-05-18/html/2012-12045.htm)). PQS is "the minimum knowledge and skills that an individual must demonstrate in order to qualify to stand watches," and standards carry NAVEDTRA numbers such as 43512-2 ([NAVEDTRA 43512-2](https://navalsafetycommand.navy.mil/Portals/100/Documents/PQS_43512-2_Personnel_Working_Aloft.pdf)). Watchstation line items use numbers such as 2200 Lookout, 2300 Helmsman, 2400 QMOW and 2500 BMOW. These examples are hosted by a Sea Cadet unit, but they mirror the Navy numbering ([2200 Lookout](https://nscsgrayfox.org/Documents/PQS/2200%20Lookout.pdf)). NECs have been **four alphanumeric characters since October 2017**; for example, legacy 2800 became S000 ([Navy Times](https://www.navytimes.com/news/your-navy/2017/07/13/navy-overhauls-enlisted-rating-structure-and-nec-codes/)). A 2024 MyNavy HR guide reportedly says R-ADM data "is not accepted by NSIPS and is not documented in the ESR," and that disconnected afloat data is refreshed through "replication" ([NAVPERS 15878N](https://www.mynavyhr.navy.mil/Portals/55/Career/CareerCounseling/CCC%20NAVPERS%2015878N%20-%20UPDATE.pdf)). Shipboard qual records and shore records can therefore legitimately diverge, which is a realistic data-quality scenario worth simulating. NSIPS Web Afloat remained in use in 2025 ([NAVADMIN 184/25](https://www.mynavyhr.navy.mil/Portals/55/Messages/NAVADMIN/NAV2025/NAV25184.pdf?ver=OKC4P-obQleuV6cWKUKbLA%3D%3D)). Whether PRD, EAOS and NEC fields sit in R-ADM is an inference; no public schema confirms it.

## Readiness flows through DRRS-N pillars, not direct feeds

The readiness architecture links these systems mostly by analysis, not by documented pipes. DRRS-N reports resource readiness across the **PESTO** pillars (Personnel, Equipment, Supply, Training, Ordnance), with Facilities added in some documents ([NTRP 1-03.5, Feb 2016](https://elearning.sabresystems.com/drrsS/instructions/NTRP%201-03.5%20DRRS-N%20REPORTING%20MANUAL%20EDITION%20FEBRUARY%202016.pdf)). For the Personnel pillar, **"FLTMPS/NTMPS is primary source"** for NEC requirements. Surface training values flow **TORIS → NTIMS → DRRS-N**, with TFOM as the core training metric ([COMNAVSURFORINST 3502.1D](https://navytribe.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/comnavsurforinst-3502-1d-1.pdf); [DRRS-S PXO brief, 2023](https://elearning.sabresystems.com/drrsS/presentations/DRRS-S%20PXO%20Presentation_01Jan2023.pdf)). No source shows R-ADM feeding DRRS-N directly, and no source shows SKED feeding TFOM or MFOM. SKED's native metric is the accomplishment rating, and GAO found that MRCs are "sometimes inaccurate with respect to the time and personnel needed" ([GAO-24-106525](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106525)). That finding argues for treating MRC man-hours as noisy estimates. Personnel strain is the other driver: the Navy needed **84,379 enlisted sailors at sea but had 70,705** in November 2023 ([Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/news/your-navy/2024/09/11/how-a-sailor-shortage-is-crippling-ship-maintenance-at-sea/)).

| Integration point | What the sources support | Status |
|---|---|---|
| NTCSS | Suite hosting R-Supply, OMMS-NG, R-ADM (and NALCOMIS) | NTCSS RDT&E zeroed from FY2020, with NOBLE funded instead ([PB2020 0604231N](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/budget/fy2020/navy-peds/U_0604231N_5_PB_2020.pdf)); no sunset date found |
| R-Supply | Operationally coupled with OMMS-NG (job postings); targeted by NOBLE NOSS | Join via requisition numbers (field name unverified) |
| SKED ↔ OMMS-NG | Interface existed; Navy stopped funding it (GAO) | No live feed; correlate analytically |
| AWN / MFOM | AWN is the MFOM shipboard segment replacing OMMS-NG | Fielded unevenly; some ships, largely West Coast, still on OMMS-NG (GAO 2024) |
| DRRS-N | PESTO pillars | Direct inputs from SKED and R-ADM unconfirmed |
| TORIS / TFOM | TORIS → NTIMS → DRRS-N training pillar | Sourced (2015–2023 documents) |
| NSIPS / FLTMPS | Shore personnel and NEC authority; R-ADM data not accepted by NSIPS | Replication-based refresh |
| STRMS | CANES-hosted watchstander proficiency and watchbill tool (Aptima/GD; NAVSEA PMS 339) | Coexists with R-ADM; whether it reads from or replaces R-ADM is unverified |

STRMS, developed by Aptima and carried by General Dynamics, tracks "individual watchstander qualification/experience/proficiency and watchbill tracking" and is hosted "locally on the ship's CANES network" ([GD STRMS](https://gdmissionsystems.com/shipboard-integration-and-combat-systems/strms); [Aptima](https://aptima.com/?p=18733)). NAVSEA PMS 339 lists it as a managed system ([NAVSEA 21](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/serve-from-netstorage/Home/Team-Ships/NAVSEA-21/Surface-Training-Systems/index.html)).

## Modernization is announced but unfinished as of November 2026

All three systems are in transition, and no source documents a completed transition. NOBLE combines NOSS (supply; One Network, $42.6M, 2020), NAMS (aviation) and NOME. Palantir provides the integrated data environment, and NTCSS is described as the largest legacy system NOBLE replaces ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200305005915/en/Palantir-Awarded-Contract-Support-U.S.-Navy-NOBLE); [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-united-states-navy-awards-43-million-contract-to-one-network-enterprises-for-the-naval-operational-business-logistics-enterprise-noble-naval-operational-supply-system-noss-301166187.html)). **N-MRO went to Lockheed Martin and IFS in May 2021** to cover NAMS and NOME across more than 3,000 assets and more than 200,000 sailors ([Seapower](https://seapowermagazine.org/navy-selects-lockheed-martin-ifs-to-deliver-intelligent-ship-aircraft-maintenance/)). RAND recorded *planned* NOME initial capability in Q4 FY2022 and full capability in Q4 FY2025. These were plans, not confirmed achievements ([RAND RR2974z1](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RR2900/RR2974z1/RAND_RR2974z1.pdf)). GAO's recommendation that the Navy communicate N-MRO's purpose remained open, with **"As of November 2025, the Navy has not taken action"** ([GAO-24-106525](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106525)). Separately, Antech says SKED, the PMS MIS and 3-M MDS "are being transformed into a cohesive application" that builds configuration-specific schedules ashore, but gives no date ([Antech Future of PMS](https://www.antechsystems.com/services/maintenance-management/future-of-the-planned-maintenance-system/)). No source ties SKED explicitly to NOBLE. No source confirms R-ADM's 2026 fielding status or names a replacement. Since NOBLE's public scope is supply and maintenance, R-ADM is probably *not* a NOBLE target; that is an inference. A realistic late-2026 fleet therefore runs a mix of OMMS-NG, AWN and early N-MRO data, which argues for schemas that do not depend on any one source system.

## Recommended synthetic files for MissionTwin

What the generator should copy, based on the sourced facts, is the set of real identifiers and relationships. The proposed headers are **the notes' own unofficial designs**. Every code value they contain is a placeholder unless the sections above cite it. Real PII must never appear: use a synthetic `member_id`, never an SSN.

| System | Recommended file(s) | Sourced anchors | Unofficial / [UNVERIFIED] elements |
|---|---|---|---|
| SKED | `sked_schedule.csv` (one row per scheduled MRC instance per week), optional `sked_loep.csv` reference | LOEP fields (force rev, TYCOM, hull, UIC, WC, MIP, equipment description); MIP periodicity, man-hours, skill level; weekly assignee; statuses complete/lost; IEM, situational/metered triggers, spot-check scores | Periodicity table, MIP/MRC numbering, WC code style, force-rev string ("2-26"), statuses beyond complete/lost |
| OMMS-NG / CSMP | `csmp.csv` (one row per JCN), `casreps.json` (one object per CASREP, many-to-one to JCN) | JCN = UIC(5)+WC(4)+JSN(4); Block 41 priority; Block 42 TA 1–4; CASREP DTG in remarks; APL/EIC/location; CO deferral signature; 2P/CK distinction; Excel-style extracts in practice | Status, deferral-reason, when-discovered and safety codes; EIC/APL formats; CASREP C2–C4 categories and report types; requisition-number field |
| R-Supply (join) | `requisitions.csv` | Coupling to OMMS-NG only | All fields |
| R-ADM | `roster.csv`, `quals.csv`, `watchbill.csv` | Duty section, division, work center; PQS (NAVEDTRA refs); qualified / interim / final; watchbill policy; 4-character NEC format; watchstation numbers 2200–2500 | Field names, status enumeration beyond the sourced states, PRD/EAOS placement |
| FLTMPS-style | `nec_billets.csv` | FLTMPS/NTMPS as NEC-requirement source | Billet table shape |

The notes propose these headers. For SKED: `ship_uic,hull,tycom,force_rev,work_center,mip,equipment_desc,egl_location,mrc,periodicity,man_hours,rate,sched_week_start,assigned_to,status,status_date,recorded_by_pin,spot_check_score,feedback_report_id,iem_flag,notes`. For the CSMP: `jcn,uic,work_center,jsn,eic,apl,equipment_noun,location,date_discovered,date_deferred,status_code,deferral_reason_code,priority,type_avail,est_man_hours,casrep_dtg,casrep_serial,casrep_category,req_doc_numbers,remarks,lead_wc,verifier,co_deferral_signed,job_state`. For R-ADM, `roster.csv` carries member_id, rate and rating, department, division, work_center, duty_section, status, report_date, prd, eaos and NECs. `quals.csv` carries member_id, pqs_ref, watchstation_code and name, status, pct_complete, interim and final dates, and requalification_due. `watchbill.csv` carries date, watch_period, condition, watchstation, section and member_id.

These records support a handful of realistic behaviors:

- Most weekly checks have D, W or M periodicity, and Q, S and A checks cluster in particular weeks.
- R-coded checks fire on events such as getting underway.
- Lost PMS on an item raises the odds of a later 2-Kilo or CASREP. This should be modeled as a correlation, not a system feed, because the SKED–OMMS-NG interface lost funding.
- A CASREP job carries its DTG in the 2-Kilo remarks.
- Watchbill assignees must have INTERIM or QUALIFIED status.
- Several holders of a critical NEC leaving within 90 days (a PRD cliff) produce a personnel shortfall.
- R-ADM and shore records drift apart, consistent with NSIPS not accepting R-ADM data.

## Conclusion

MissionTwin's biggest realism risk is false precision, not missing fields. None of the three systems publishes a digital schema. The authoritative structure lives in paper-form block numbers, in policy language about who is authoritative, and in a few identifiers with confirmed formats: the JCN, NEC and PQS watchstation numbers. A generator built on those anchors, with every code table visibly labeled as placeholder, will look credible to fleet judges, and an invented "official" layout will not. The sources also point to a better forecasting design than a single pipeline. Readiness signals in the real fleet are already fragmented: SKED is cut off from OMMS-NG, AWN is fielded unevenly, R-ADM is not accepted by NSIPS, and N-MRO's scope is still being explained to stakeholders. A read-only tool that reconciles these partial, divergent sources is closer to the Navy's actual problem than one that assumes clean feeds. MissionTwin can show that value directly by injecting divergence on purpose: lost PMS that has no matching 2-Kilo, quals present in R-ADM but missing from FLTMPS, and CASREP DTGs that match no open JCN.

The items to verify first, by someone with a PDF reader and the public NAVSEA documents, are these: NAVSEAINST 4790.8D Chapter 2 and Appendix G (periodicity codes, the no-deferral rule, 2-Kilo code tables), NTTP 1-03.1 (CASREP categories and format), and the FY2025–27 0604231N budget books (NTCSS sunset and N-MRO fielding).
